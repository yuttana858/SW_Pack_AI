# Print processes and their effect on board performance

## Process overview

| Process | Where ink goes | Typical use | Board-strength effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexo post-print** | Water-based ink from a relief plate onto finished corrugated board [S2] | Shipping cases, 1–4 colour line work; newer presses do higher-colour graphics [S1] | Highest crush risk: the printing nip presses the fluted board (see below) |
| **Flexo pre-print** | Liner reels printed flat *before* the corrugator [S1] | Long, repeating high-graphic runs; smooth surface, no flute shadow [S1] | Board is not crushed by printing; the corrugator still sets caliper |
| **Litho-lamination** | Offset-printed sheet laminated to corrugated. IP describes up to six colours, 150–230 line screen [S3] | Retail and display, premium graphics. Single-face lam for high volume, double-face lam for smaller orders [S3] | No print nip on the fluted board; the lamination step and adhesive water can affect flatness |
| **Digital inkjet (single-pass)** | Water-based inkjet, either direct to board or onto liner reels (for example HP T1190: six colours, up to 305 m/min [1000 ft/min]) [S4][S5] | Short runs, versioning, pre-print without plates | No-contact print: no plate nip. Ink and bonding-agent water still wets the liner |
| **Offset (sheetfed litho) for folding cartons** | Conventional, UV or LED-UV inks onto paperboard sheets [S21] | Folding cartons, luxury and cosmetic packs [S21] | Not a corrugated crush issue. Watch scoring and folding cracks on heavy coatings |

## Anilox rolls (flexo)

The anilox meters ink to the plate. It is specified by **screen** (cells per inch, lpi, or per cm, l/cm) and **volume** (BCM, or cm³/m²). Finer screens carry less ink.

| Application | Screen | Volume | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sheetfed corrugated, coarse to fine (60° cells) | 200 lpi (78.7 l/cm) to 400 lpi (157.5 l/cm) | 9.5 BCM to 4.5 BCM | [S6] |
| Common corrugated example | 250 lpi (98.4 l/cm) | 7.5 BCM | [S6] |
| Narrow web, wide web and pre-print (60°) | 360 to 1400 lpi | 5.0 to 1.0 BCM | [S6] |
| Solids / type / halftones (general guide) | — | 5–9 / 3–6 / 1–4 BCM | [S7] |

Conversions (derived): l/cm = lpi / 2.54. If BCM means 10⁹ µm³ of cell volume per in², then 1 BCM = 1.550003 cm³/m² (so 7.5 BCM ≈ 11.63 cm³/m²). Use the anilox supplier's chart for screen-to-volume pairing, and check the BCM definition with the supplier (VERIFY). Volume falls as cells wear, so audit rolls periodically.

## Flexo plates for corrugated post-print

Soft, thick plates absorb flute and caliper variation and reduce "washboarding" (flute show-through).

| Plate | Thickness | Hardness | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| IP Translite | 3.94 mm (0.155 in) / 6.35 mm (0.250 in) | 28 / 26 Shore A | [S8] |
| IP Transcorr | 3.94 mm / 6.35 mm | 36 Shore A | [S9] |
| Flint nyloflex FSC Digital | includes 3.94 mm | 26 Shore A | [S10] |

Pattern: corrugated post-print plates sit in roughly the mid-20s to mid-30s Shore A in these data sheets [S8][S9][S10]. Durometer depends on the measurement stack and method, so compare like with like (VERIFY with the plate supplier).

## Ink types

- **Water-based**: the standard for corrugated flexo post-print [S2] and for single-pass inkjet such as the HP A30 inks [S4]. HP states that its water-based inks contain no UV-reactive chemistry and suit food packaging [S4] (vendor claim). PTS testing reported in the trade press found no optically disturbing inhomogeneities in recycled pulp from HP digital prints [S26].
- **UV (mercury lamp)**: instant cure, high gloss and rub resistance. Lamps produce ozone, which usually requires exhaust [S21].
- **LED-UV**: narrow-band lamps, typically 385 nm or 395 nm, needing inks made for that wavelength. No mercury and no ozone. One supplier quotes lamp life of about 20 000 h against about 2000 h for mercury lamps [S21] (vendor figure, VERIFY). Widely used for sheetfed folding cartons [S21].
- **Food contact**: low-migration ink systems, or a functional barrier, are needed under EU Framework Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 obligations [S22]. Get the ink maker's migration statement for the specific substrate and end use.

## Effect of printing on board strength (crush)

Mechanism: in post-print, the printing nip (plate pressure, anilox and feed rolls) partly crushes the flutes. That lowers caliper, which in turn lowers ECT and BCT. One patent links caliper loss from printing crush to loss of compression strength in C-flute board [S11]. Lighter and higher-recycled-content boards make it harder to achieve solid coverage without crush, and lower print pressure helps [S25].

Reported magnitudes (conflicting, so VERIFY):

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| US patent 4544597 | Printing after board manufacture may cost **10 % to 20 %** of strength (industry belief cited in the patent) [S11] |
| RIT thesis (1990) | Tested 15 % and 30 % print coverage, number of colours, panels printed and flute. *Some* printing factors had a significant effect on RSC compression strength; percentages are not in the abstract [S12] |
| RIT thesis (1998) | Thick-plate flexo significantly reduced compression strength compared with pre-printed or thin-plate printed containers. More colours with thick plates meant more loss [S13] |
| Vendor case study | About 50 % compression loss after printing that damaged the medium (anecdote, not a controlled test) [S14] |

```
BCT_printed = BCT_unprinted × (1 − L_print)
```

- `BCT_unprinted`: predicted or measured BCT of the unprinted box (N) [lbf]
- `L_print`: fractional loss from printing (–). Use a measured house value from `company/`. Without one, bracket 0.10–0.20 [S11] and flag it.

Worked example, SI: BCT_unprinted = 5000 N; L_print = 0.10 to 0.20 gives BCT_printed = **4500 N to 4000 N**.
Worked example, US: BCT_unprinted = 1124 lbf (5000 N); BCT_printed = **1011.6 lbf to 899.2 lbf**.

Limits: loss depends on plate thickness and hardness, impression setting, coverage and its location (print near score lines and corners matters most), number of print units, flute and board grade. Measure ECT and caliper before and after printing on the real press. Derived formula.

## Coverage, varnishes and coatings

- **Coverage** is the share of a panel area carrying ink. Heavy solids need more volume (anilox BCM) and impression, which increases crush risk in post-print [S6][S25]. Coverage levels of 15 % and 30 % were study variables in [S12].
- **Varnishes and coatings** (water-based overprint varnish, UV or LED-UV varnish, functional barrier coatings) add rub and scuff resistance or moisture resistance. Coatings also affect repulpability and recyclability: see `kb.sustainability.regulations-and-recyclability`. No sourced numeric data on strength effects was found (VERIFY with the coating supplier).

## Barcode print quality: ITF-14 on corrugated

- The ITF-14 nominal (100 %) X-dimension is 1.016 mm (0.040 in). 62.5 % magnification gives X = 0.635 mm (0.025 in). Symbols with X below 0.635 mm should be used only on labels or high-quality substrates [S16].
- Print a **bearer box** when printing directly on corrugated [S16]. One retailer specification asks for horizontal bearer bars twice the X-dimension thick [S17] (VERIFY against the GS1 General Specifications).
- ISO/IEC 15416 grades: per Axicon, the minimum pass grade is **1.5 (C)** for most symbols. For ITF-14 with X ≥ 0.635 mm, typically printed on brown board, it is **0.5 (D)** [S15]. A 3M retailer specification requires 1.5/C for labels and non-corrugated packages [S17]. **VERIFY**: confirm the current GS1 General Specifications table and your customer's requirement. Retailers may be stricter.
- Quiet zones: one secondary source gives a 10X minimum each side, not confirmed from GS1 (VERIFY).
- Practical points: run bars in the machine (flow) direction where possible, so flexo slur stretches bars rather than widening them. Avoid print over flute peaks, scores and flaps. Verify on press with a calibrated ISO/IEC 15416 verifier (not a scanner read test).

## Colour management basics

- **G7** is a calibration method that adjusts device curves to a target neutral print density curve (NPDC) and grey balance. It is formalised in CGATS TR 015 and is a specification to help meet standards, not an ISO standard [S18][S20]. It has been applied to flexography [S18]. Idealliance's test forms may be too large for narrow-web flexo, so flexo printers can build their own [S19].
- **ISO 12647-6** is the process-control standard for flexographic printing. The ink colour standard it references has been withdrawn, so printers are often advised to target a published characterisation (such as GRACoL 2013) [S19] (VERIFY current edition).
- Post-print on brown kraft or mottled white liners cannot reach the same colour gamut as coated white top liners or litho-lam. Agree on proof conditions (substrate, ink set, curve) with the printer.

## Dielines and bleed for print files

- Keep the **dieline** (cut, crease or score, perforation) on its own named, non-printing layer [S24]. Use the converter's dieline for the exact board and flute, since manufacturing dimensions differ from inside dimensions (see `dimensional/`).
- **Bleed**: about 3 mm (1/8 in) beyond the cut line is usual for folding cartons. Corrugated printers often ask for more, so confirm with the converter [S23][S24] (VERIFY with the converter's spec).
- **Safe zone**: keep text and critical art at least about 3 mm (1/8 in) inside cut and score lines [S23]. Increase this on corrugated, where registration tolerance is wider.
- State the dimension basis (inside, outside or manufacturing) and the order (L × W × D) on the artwork brief.
