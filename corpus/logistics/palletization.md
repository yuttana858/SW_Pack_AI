# Palletization, unit loads and cube utilisation

Values in this file are **industry-typical** unless a standard is cited. Pallet ratings depend on the specific design, timber and condition: *replace with supplier/house data in `company/`*. Formulas and worked examples are **derived**. Notes on research access: on 2026-11-09 the publishers' pages were reached through search-engine summaries only, because the session could not open the pages directly. Every value below should be checked against the cited document before release (see VERIFY markers).

## Pallet footprint standards

ISO 6780:2003 sets principal dimensions and tolerances for new flat pallets for intercontinental handling, of any material, single- or double-deck, reversible or non-reversible [S1]. It replaced ISO 6780:1988 and is confirmed as current [S1]. Secondary sources say the standard recognises six footprints, but they disagree on which six [S25][S26]. Most list 1219 × 1016 mm, 1200 × 1000 mm, 1200 × 800 mm, 1140 × 1140 mm and 1100 × 1100 mm [S25]. For the remaining slot, sources variously give 1067 × 1067 mm, 800 × 600 mm or 1165 × 1165 mm. A CHEP article states that 1165 × 1165 mm is in ISO 6780 [S26], while other sources give 1140 × 1140 mm for Australia [S25]. **VERIFY** against the ISO text before quoting the six sizes as normative.

| Footprint (mm) | Footprint (in) | Common name / region | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1219 × 1016 | 48 × 40 | GMA / North American grocery | ISO 6780 size [S1]; CHEP block and stringer pools [S4][S5] |
| 1200 × 800 | 47.2 × 31.5 | EUR / EPAL 1 (Euro pallet) | 144 mm high, about 25 kg [S2] |
| 1200 × 1000 | 47.2 × 39.4 | EPAL 3 / "ISO 1" / UK and industrial | EPAL 3 is about 30 kg [S3] |
| 1100 × 1100 | 43.3 × 43.3 | Asia (Japan, Korea) | Listed in ISO 6780 per secondary sources [S25] (VERIFY) |
| 1165 × 1165 | 45.9 × 45.9 | Australia / New Zealand (CHEP) | 150 mm high, tare about 38 kg [S6]; stated as an ISO 6780 size [S26] (VERIFY) |

Dimension order: pallet footprint is quoted **length × width**, with length along the stringers or top-deck boards per the pallet supplier's drawing. State which side the forks enter (two-way or four-way) when it matters for the load pattern.

## Pallet load capacity: static, dynamic and racked

Three ratings exist because the pallet is supported differently in each case:

- **Static**: load resting on a flat, level floor. Highest rating.
- **Dynamic**: load carried on forklift tines or a pallet jack.
- **Racked**: load spanning rack beams (edge-supported). Usually the lowest, and some pallets are not rated for it.

Published values (manufacturer or pool data, uniformly distributed load):

| Pallet | Rating stated | Value (SI) | Value (US) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EPAL Euro 1200 × 800 | Safe working load | 1500 kg | 3307 lb | [S2] |
| EPAL Euro 1200 × 800 | Max load on bottom pallet when block-stacked on solid level floor | 5500 kg | 12125 lb | [S2] |
| EPAL 3 1200 × 1000 | Safe working load / stacking limit | 1500 kg / 4500 kg | 3307 lb / 9921 lb | [S3] |
| CHEP 48 × 40 block (North America) | Design load, normal operation | 1270 kg | 2800 lb | [S4] |
| CHEP 48 × 40 block | Block-stack limit, up to 5 unit loads high | 4536 kg | 10000 lb | [S4] |
| CHEP B4840A 48 × 40 | Recommended uniformly distributed load | 1270 kg | 2800 lb | [S5] |
| Wood stringer GMA 48 × 40 (one spec sheet) | Static / dynamic / racked | 2722 / 998 / 907 kg | 6000 / 2200 / 2000 lb | [S8] |
| CHEP Australian 1165 × 1165 | Drive-in racking payload | 2000 kg | 4409 lb | [S6] |
| CHEP Australian 1165 × 1165 | Static load | 10000 kg [S6] vs 5000 kg [S7] | 22046 lb vs 11023 lb | **VERIFY**: sources disagree |

US values are converted from the SI or US value the source gave (1 kg = 2.204623 lb; see `kb.reference.units-and-conversions`). "GMA" describes a footprint, not a strength grade. Two pallets with the same 48 × 40 in plan can have very different ratings depending on block versus stringer construction, timber and fasteners. Always use the rating of the actual pallet, take the lowest rating that applies to the handling chain (racked if any racking is involved), and record the source.

## Maximum unit load height and vehicle interior dimensions

The height limit for a unit load is the lowest of: customer or DC specification, racking beam clearance, vehicle **door opening** height (not interior height), and stability or compression limits. Interior dimensions vary by builder and model, so use the actual equipment's plate or spec sheet.

| Equipment | Inside L × W × H (mm) | Door W × H (mm) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 ft dry container | 5898 × 2350 × 2390 | 2340 × 2280 | [S10] |
| 40 ft dry container | about 12032 × 2350 × 2390 | width 2340 | [S11] |
| 40 ft high cube | 12032 × 2350 × 2700 | 2340 × 2597 | [S9] |
| 53 ft dry van (US) | 16002 × 2565 × 2794 (630 × 101 × 110 in) | height 2791 (109-7/8 in) | length [S13]; width, height, door [S12] |

Container payloads also limit the load: for example ONE lists 28330 kg (62457 lb) payload for its 20 ft dry container [S10], and Hapag-Lloyd lists 28600 kg (63052 lb) for its 40 ft high cube at 32500 kg gross [S9]. Carrier figures differ by a few per cent [S9][S10][S11]. Trailer width varies by model (some vans are 98.5 in lining to lining) [S12]. Road axle and gross-weight limits usually govern before cube for dense goods; check them separately.

### Pallets per vehicle (single-orientation grid estimate)

```
n_floor = floor(W_in / a) × floor(L_in / b)
```

- `W_in`, `L_in`: inside width and length of the vehicle (mm) [in]
- `a`, `b`: pallet side placed across the width and along the length (mm) [in]

Worked example, US: 53 ft van, 630 × 101 in inside [S12][S13], 48 × 40 in pallets.
- 48 in across: floor(101/48) × floor(630/40) = 2 × 15 = **30** pallets.
- 40 in across: floor(101/40) × floor(630/48) = 2 × 13 = **26** pallets.

Worked example, SI: 40 ft container, 12032 × 2350 mm inside [S9], EUR 1200 × 800 mm pallets.
- 800 mm across: floor(2350/800) × floor(12032/1200) = 2 × 10 = **20** pallets.
- 1200 mm across: 1 × 15 = **15** pallets.

Limitations: this ignores mixed orientations (which often fit more pallets in containers), door clearance, wall corrugations, load-securing gaps, and axle weights. Use load-planning software or the carrier's loading guide for final counts. Derived values.

## Pallet patterns

| Pattern | Description | Strength | Stability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Column** (aligned) | Every tier identical; case corners sit on case corners below | Highest stacking strength, because the load runs down the corners and side walls [S23] | Lowest; columns can separate. Needs stretch wrap, tier sheets or corner boards |
| **Interlock / brick** | Alternate tiers rotated or mirrored so cases bridge the joints below | Lower: DHL states interlocking can cut compression strength by up to 50 % when the contents are not rigid [S17] (VERIFY: guidance figure, not a test result) | Higher; tiers lock together [S23] |
| **Pinwheel** | Cases arranged around a central gap, each tier rotated 90° | Similar penalty to interlock; leaves a central void | Good with square footprints |
| **Hybrid** | Column-stack the lower tiers, interlock the top one or two tiers | Keeps most of the compression strength where the load is highest [S23] | Locks the top of the load |

Misalignment counts too. A Michigan State thesis cites McKee (1963): each 1 % of offset costs about 2.5 % of box compression strength [S20]. That applies to tiers offset within a column stack, not to overhang (VERIFY against the original).

## Area and cube utilisation

```
U_area = (n × l × w) / (L_p × W_p) × 100 %
U_cube = (N_cases × l × w × h) / (L_p × W_p × H_load) × 100 %
tiers  = floor((H_max − h_p) / h)
```

- `n`: cases per layer (–)
- `l`, `w`, `h`: case outside length, width and depth (mm) [in]. Use **outside** dimensions.
- `L_p`, `W_p`: pallet length and width (mm) [in]
- `N_cases`: total cases on the pallet (–)
- `H_load`: height of the product stack above the deck (mm) [in] for the load's own cube, or the allowed height above the deck for the "available cube". State which one you use [S24].
- `H_max`: maximum unit-load height including the pallet (mm) [in]
- `h_p`: pallet height (mm) [in], measured

Worked example, US: 16 × 12 × 10 in outside cases on a 48 × 40 in pallet, `H_max` = 60 in, `h_p` = 6.0 in (assumed for illustration; measure the real pallet).
- Lengthwise: floor(48/16) × floor(40/12) = 3 × 3 = 9 cases. U_area = 9 × 192 / 1920 = **90.0 %**.
- Rotated: floor(48/12) × floor(40/16) = 4 × 2 = 8 cases, 80.0 %.
- Tiers = floor((60 − 6)/10) = 5. Load height = 6 + 50 = 56 in. U_cube (to the stack top) = 45 × 1920 / (1920 × 50) = **90.0 %**.

Worked example, SI: 380 × 285 × 250 mm outside cases on a EUR 1200 × 800 mm pallet (h_p = 144 mm [S2]), `H_max` = 1500 mm.
- 285 mm along the 1200 mm side: floor(1200/285) × floor(800/380) = 4 × 2 = 8 cases. U_area = 8 × 0.10830 / 0.96 = **90.25 %**.
- Other orientation: 3 × 2 = 6 cases, 67.7 %.
- Tiers = floor((1500 − 144)/250) = 5. Load height = 144 + 1250 = 1394 mm.

Target utilisation: none of the sources consulted gives a universal target. Use a company target in `company/` and weigh it against damage rate, stability and compression; stability often limits height before cube does (VERIFY; no sourced benchmark). The calculation is derived.

Limitations: the formula counts only simple grid patterns. Mixed-block patterns (some cases rotated within one layer) can do better, and pallet-pattern software handles these. Using inside dimensions overstates utilisation.

## Overhang and underhang

- **Overhang** (cases beyond the pallet edge) is to be avoided. The cases' side walls lose support, and the cases are exposed to handling damage. DHL states that overhang can reduce individual box strength by more than 30 % and that boxes should not overhang the pallet edge [S17].
- Virginia Tech work for the FBA found that box compression strength falls as overhang increases. One Phase 1 summary shows losses of about 13.78 % to 28.21 % [S18]. The test conditions behind that range were not visible in the summary consulted (**VERIFY**). Follow-up modelling found that the amount of overhang, box perimeter and board grade drive the loss [S19].
- **Underhang** (load smaller than the deck) wastes cube and can let the load shift. No sourced numeric limit was found. Use customer or DC specifications in `company/` (VERIFY).

Engineering rule: if overhang cannot be avoided, apply a measured or modelled derating to box compression strength [S18][S19] and say so in the answer.

## Load stability and containment

Main methods (descriptive; no sourced performance values):

- **Stretch wrap** (machine or hand): containment force and wrap pattern set by the film supplier. Anchor the film to the pallet.
- **Corner boards / edge protectors**: spread strap pressure, protect corners and add some column support.
- **Tier sheets** (corrugated or solid board between layers): spread load across case joints and stabilise column stacks.
- **Slip sheets**: replace the pallet for push-pull handling. They need compatible lift trucks at both ends.
- **Strapping, glue or anti-slip coatings** between tiers.

Test for horizontal rigidity: **EUMOS 40509** quantifies the horizontal rigidity of unit loads in road transport on an acceleration bench [S21][S22]. The 2020 revision added evaluation criteria [S22]. One test lab states acceptance limits of no more than 5 % permanent and 10 % dynamic deformation [S22] (VERIFY against EUMOS 40509:2020). It is referenced through the EU roadworthiness directive 2014/47/EU [S22].

## Pallet test standards (summary)

- **ASTM D1185**: performance of pallets and related structures used as skids, bases, platforms and bins. Covers conditioning, static stiffness and strength tests and dynamic tests of structural reliability. Also classifies pallets as single- or multiple-use and sets safe working loads. The ASTM store lists D1185-98a(2025) as active [S14]. Other listings disagree about the current status (VERIFY).
- **ISO 8611-1**: test methods for flat pallets, grouped into nominal-load, maximum-working-load and durability-comparison tests. Evaluating against requirements needs ISO/TS 8611-2 (performance requirements and test selection). Part 3 covers maximum working loads [S15]. ISO's catalogue lists a 2025 edition as current, with 2004, 2011 and 2021 withdrawn (VERIFY the publication status) [S15].
- **ISO 15629:2002**: *not* a pallet performance test. It gives guidance on the quality of **fasteners** (nails and staples) used to assemble new and repair used wooden flat pallets [S16]. It is listed as confirmed in 2016 [S16]. Do not cite it for load ratings.

Point engineers to the standard text for procedures. This corpus summarises the scope only.

## Stacking load on the bottom case and the link to BCT

The palletised load sets the compression demand on the bottom-tier cases. It feeds the BCT requirement in the performance files (`performance/` stacking calculations).

```
F_bottom = g × [ (N − 1) × m_case + (S − 1) × (N × k × m_case + m_pallet) / k ]
BCT_required = F_bottom × SF / (1 − L_pattern)
```

- `F_bottom`: static load on one bottom-tier case (N) [lbf]
- `g`: 9.80665 m/s²
- `m_case`: gross mass of one filled case (kg) [lb]
- `N`: tiers per pallet (–)
- `k`: cases per layer (–)
- `S`: unit loads stacked high in the warehouse or vehicle (–). Use 1 if not stacked.
- `m_pallet`: mass of the pallet (plus tier sheets and top cap) of each upper unit load (kg) [lb]
- `SF`: safety factor for time, humidity, handling and so on, taken from the environmental-factor files in `performance/`. It is not set here.
- `L_pattern`: fractional strength loss from pattern, overhang or misalignment (–), from [S17][S18][S20] or measured.

Assumes: uniform load sharing across the `k` cases, a rigid pallet deck, and that the top unit load's pallet bears evenly on the cases below. Real pallets deflect, which concentrates load on the cases over blocks or stringers.

Worked example, SI: m_case = 10 kg, N = 6, k = 8, S = 2, m_pallet = 25 kg (EPAL weight [S2]).
- F_bottom = 9.80665 × [5 × 10 + 1 × (6 × 8 × 10 + 25)/8] = 9.80665 × 113.125 = **1109.4 N**.
- With an illustrative SF = 5 (not a recommendation) and L_pattern = 0.20 (overhang within the range reported in [S18]): BCT_required = 1109.4 × 5 / 0.80 = **6933.6 N**.

Worked example, US: m_case = 22 lb, N = 6, k = 9, S = 2, m_pallet = 45 lb (assumed; weigh the real pallet).
- F_bottom = 5 × 22 + (6 × 9 × 22 + 45)/9 = 110 + 137 = **247.0 lbf** (1098.7 N).
- With SF = 5 (illustrative) and L_pattern = 0.20: BCT_required = 247 × 5 / 0.80 = **1543.8 lbf**.

Limits: this is a static estimate. It does not cover dynamic loads in transit, creep, or uneven pallet support. Confirm with a compression test (ASTM D642 or equivalent) and a distribution test (ISTA) before release. Derived.
