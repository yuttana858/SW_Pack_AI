# Machine capability reference

This file lists, for each machine kind in `schemas/machine_spec.schema.json` [S65], what the machine does, which capability limits decide whether a design can run on it, typical ranges with **example** models, and how each limit maps to a `machine_spec` field. Its job is to seed future per-plant `machine_spec` records (`scope: plant:<id>` or `machine:<plant>/<machine>`) and the design feasibility check described in `docs/DATA-FRAMEWORK.md` §7.

> **Industry-typical values. Replace with supplier/house data in `company/`.** Every value below is a maker's catalogue figure for one configuration. Catalogue limits are "depending on material properties" and configuration [S1][S10]. The machine your plant actually runs, with its options, wear and board, sets the real envelope. Model names are examples, not endorsements.

## How to read these tables

- **Sheet vs. blank vs. box.** Feeders limit the *sheet* (or blank) size. Folding sections limit *panel* and *folded box* sizes. Check both.
- **Direction.** Most limits are given as "across the machine × in the running direction". BOBST FFG and DRO sheets: `L` (or "sheet width" on the DRO) is across the machine and holds the panels; `l` is in the running direction and holds flap + depth + flap [S2][S8]. Folder-gluer blanks: `C` is across the machine (blank width), `E` is in the running direction (blank length), `L` is the width of the flat folded box, and `H` is the wall height of a 4- or 6-corner tray [S16][S18]. The letter meanings are read from the maker's drawings; confirm on the plant machine (**VERIFY**).
- **Speed units differ by machine kind.** Corrugators and folder-gluers quote m/min; FFGs quote boxes/h; die cutters, presses and laminators quote sheets/h. The schema has one field, `max_speed_per_min`, with no unit. See [Proposed schema fields](#proposed-schema-fields).
- **Search-summary sources.** Sources marked "search-result summary" could not be opened from this environment. Their numbers come from search-engine extracts of the maker or dealer page and are marked **VERIFY**.
- Cross-references: tolerances in `kb.dimensional.tolerances`; glue lap and slot width in `kb.dimensional.blank-size-formulas`; score profiles and die-cut rules in `kb.manufacturing.die-cutting-and-tooling`; process and defects in `kb.manufacturing.corrugator-and-converting`.

## Corrugator (single facer, double backer, slitter-scorer, cut-off)

### What it does

The wet end combines medium and liners. The **single facer** flutes the medium and bonds it to one liner. The **double backer** (double facer) bonds the second liner and dries the board on hot plates. The dry end makes sheets: the **slitter-scorer** slits the web into lanes and puts the longitudinal scores (the flap scores of an RSC blank) into it, and the **cut-off** (sheeter) cuts each lane to length. The stacker then delivers the sheets. See `kb.manufacturing.corrugator-and-converting` for process detail.

### Parameters that decide design feasibility

### Design notes

## Rotary die cutter (RDC)

### What it does

An RDC feeds sheets between a die cylinder (curved plywood die with steel rule) and an urethane anvil, usually with flexo print units in front. It cuts complex shapes, slots, handholes and creases in one continuous rotation. It is faster than a flatbed but less accurate; FEFCO/VDW applies the same ±0.5 % (min ±1 mm) die-cut tolerance with a looser AQL [S63] (see `kb.manufacturing.die-cutting-and-tooling`).

## Design feasibility checklist

Run this against the `machine_spec` of each machine in the route (corrugator → printer/converter → folder-gluer). Report the first limit that fails and the lines that pass.

1. **Blank fits the machine envelope.** Blank (or sheet with trim, gripper margin and multi-up layout) is within `sheet_min_mm` and `sheet_max_mm` in the correct axis order. On the corrugator, sheet width ≤ working width and sheet length ≥ minimum cut length [S10][S35][S40].
2. **Caliper and flute in range.** Board caliper within `caliper_range_mm`; flute (and wall) in `flutes`; for carton, grammage in range [S1][S12].
3. **Minimum panel and flap sizes.** FFG: small and large panels, box height, slot length and glue lap inside the panel limits [S2][S4][S6]. Corrugator: flap depth ≥ minimum score spacing; blank width ≥ minimum slit width [S35][S39]. Folder-gluer: folded width `L` and tray wall height `H` inside the per-style limits [S16][S18].
4. **Print register vs. graphics tolerance.** Artwork tolerance (trap, hairlines next to scores, print-to-edge) is wider than the machine's register: FEFCO/VDW ±2 mm for flexo [S63]; laminator ±0.25–1.0 mm depending on speed [S20]. If the graphics need tighter register than the line holds, move to litho-lam or digital, or add bleed and keep critical elements away from scores and cuts.
5. **Print area and repeat.** Print stays inside `print_area_max_mm` and within the print-cylinder repeat [S2][S8]; colours ≤ `print_units`.
6. **Joint type available.** Glued, stitched or taped joint is in `joint_types` on the converting line; joint gap and fishtail targets are within the machine's tolerances (`kb.dimensional.tolerances`).
7. **Crash-lock / 4- and 6-corner capability.** The folder-gluer has the module, and the blank meets the module's `C`, `E`, `L`, `H` minimums [S16][S17][S18].
8. **Die-cut details.** Die type available (rotary or flatbed); details respect the die-cut tolerance ±0.5 % (min ±1 mm) [S63] and the die-making rules in `kb.manufacturing.die-cutting-and-tooling`.
9. **Folded thickness.** Flat-folded box ≤ `max_folded_thickness_mm` [S16][S18].
10. **Sample matches production.** If a CAD-table sample was used for approval, note that table creases differ from production scores.
