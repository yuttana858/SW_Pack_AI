# Distribution (transit) testing

## Overview: why distribution testing and which route

Distribution testing checks that a product and its package survive the hazards of a shipping route: handling drops and impacts, compression in stacks, vehicle vibration, and climate. Two families dominate:

- **ISTA procedures** — fixed, prescriptive sequences. Retailers and carriers (Amazon, FedEx, Sam's Club) reference them, and ISTA certifies laboratories [S1][S2].
- **ASTM D4169** — a configurable practice. You choose a distribution cycle (DC), an assurance level (AL) and the test schedules apply in a set order. It is widely used for medical-device and pharmaceutical validation [S15][S16].

ISO 4180 gives general rules for compiling an ISO-route test schedule; see `testing/standards-index.md`.

Decision rule of thumb: if a customer or carrier names a procedure (e.g. Amazon → ISTA 6-Amazon.com), use exactly that. Otherwise, use ISTA 3-Series for consumer goods sent by parcel, LTL or truckload, and ASTM D4169 when a regulator or customer expects a documented, risk-based validation. The test plan is the engineer's choice; this file does not replace the procedures, which must be bought from ISTA and ASTM.

## ISTA series structure and when to pick each

| Series | Type | Purpose | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non-simulation integrity | Screening of product–package robustness; does not simulate transport | Early design screening, quick comparisons [S1][S2] |
| 2 | Partial simulation | Series 1 elements plus at least one general-simulation element such as atmospheric conditioning; not predictive of shipping performance | Refining preliminary designs [S2] |
| 3 | General simulation | General simulation of the hazards of a defined shipment type (parcel, LTL, truckload, DC-to-store) | Design verification for a known channel [S1] |
| 4 | Enhanced simulation | General simulation plus at least one focused-simulation element | Channels with known field data [S2] |
| 6 | Member performance | Protocols written by or with a member company: currently 6-Amazon.com (SIOC, Over Boxing) and 6-SAMSCLUB. The 6-FEDEX-A and -B tests were withdrawn in 2024 and 2025 | When that company requires it [S1][S40] |
| 7 | Development | Development tests, mainly 7D (thermal transport packaging) | Cold-chain shipper development [S2] |

Series 1 to 3 are generally limited to packaged-products of 150 lb (68 kg) or less, with separate "B" procedures for heavier items [S2].

Selection by shipment type (3-Series):
- **Parcel delivery, ≤70 kg (150 lb)** → ISTA 3A [S5].
- **Less-than-truckload (LTL)** → ISTA 3B [S8].
- **Similar products unitised on pallets, truckload** → ISTA 3E [S7].
- **Distribution centre to retail outlet, mixed pallets, ≤ 45 kg (100 lb) per package** → ISTA 3F [S1][S42].
- Other 3-Series options listed by ISTA: 3H (mechanically handled bulk containers), 3K (European FMCG retail), 3L (generalised e-commerce retailer fulfilment), 3M (international direct import, Asia-Pacific to US), 3N (European full-truckload unit loads, new 2026) and 3P (India parcel, new 2026) [S1][S40].
- Amazon.com fulfilment → ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC or -Over Boxing (see Amazon section).

## ISTA 3A key sequence (high level)

ISTA 3A covers individual packaged-products of 70 kg (150 lb) or less shipped through a parcel delivery system, by air or ground [S3]. The current edition is 2018 (last technical and editorial change March 2018) [S3][S40]. It defines four package types: **standard**, **small** (under 13 000 cm³ [800 in³], longest side ≤ 350 mm [14 in], ≤ 4.5 kg [10 lb]), **flat** and **elongated**; a package that is both flat and elongated is tested as elongated [S3]. One sample is the minimum; ISTA recommends two or more for fragile or liquid products and five or more for better statistics [S3].

Sequence for **standard, flat and elongated** packages, as summarised from the ISTA overview [S3]:

1. **Atmospheric preconditioning** at ambient laboratory conditions (required).
2. **Atmospheric conditioning** at a chosen temperature and humidity (optional).
3. **Shock — first drop sequence: 9 drops**, height set by packaged-product weight (required).
4. **Random vibration** with and without top load, overall levels **0.53 Grms and 0.46 Grms** (required).
5. **Random vibration under low pressure** (simulated altitude; truck or truck-and-air) (optional).
6. **Shock — second drop sequence: 8 drops**, height set by weight, including a drop onto a hazard block (required).
7. Flat and elongated only: rotational edge drop from 200 mm (8 in) and full rotational flat drop.
8. Flat only: concentrated impact (hazard box dropped 400 mm [16 in]); elongated only: bridge impact (hazard box dropped 400 mm [16 in]).
9. Liquids only: leak test, 8 hours (added in 2018) [S3][S40].

For **small** packages the order is the same, but the second drop sequence is **7 drops** with the package inside a bag of dunnage packages, and vibration uses a consolidation bag with a 36 kg (80 lb) top-load bag [S3].

So the total is 17 drops (9 + 8) for a standard package and 16 drops (9 + 7) for a small package [S3]. Earlier secondary summaries quoting "9 drops" or "8 drops" were each describing one of the two drop blocks [S4][S5]. Drop heights and orientations are in the purchased procedure.

Pass/fail is defined by the shipper (product damage tolerance and package condition) before testing, not by ISTA [S3].

## Other ISTA 3-Series parameters seen in published sources

- **ISTA 3E** (similar packaged-products in unitised loads, truckload), 2026 edition: incline or horizontal impact at 1.2 m/s (48 in/s), rotational edge drops with height set by weight, compression (machine apply-and-release at the calculated test force × 1.4, or apply-and-hold / weight and load spreader at the calculated force), random vibration at 0.54 Grms overall, then a second rotational edge drop block [S41]. The January 2026 revision returned to the earlier compression formula (largest of warehouse or vehicle stack), made the pallet on top of the sample optional and changed the impact order [S40]. The weight-dependent edge-drop height dates from the April 2017 revision [S6][S40].
- **ISTA 3B** (LTL), 2017 edition: random vibration with top load at 0.54 Grms overall [S8].
- **ISTA 3F** (2017): individual packaged-products of 45 kg (100 lb) or less shipped in mixed pallet loads, typically DC to retail store [S1][S42].
- **ISTA 3K** (2011): FMCG in the European retail chain; its mixed-load random vibration is 0.28 Grms overall [S42].

## ISTA 6-Amazon.com and Amazon packaging certification (2025–2026)

**Tiers.** Amazon's certification has three tiers [S12]:

| Tier | Name | Overbox by Amazon? | ISTA test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Frustration-Free Packaging (FFP) — easy to open, recyclable, ships without overbox | No | ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC |
| Tier 2 | Ships in Own Container (SIOC) — ships in its own package without overbox | No | ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC |
| Tier 3 | Prep-Free Packaging (PFP) — needs no Amazon prep but is shipped inside an Amazon box | Yes | ISTA 6-Amazon.com-Over Boxing |

Amazon now markets certified own-container packaging as "Ships in Product Packaging" (**SIPP**); the FFP/SIOC/PFP terms still appear in programme documents [S11][S12]. **VERIFY — tier mapping.** Secondary sources disagree on whether SIPP replaces only FFP or is the umbrella name for FFP and SIOC (both still use ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC); confirm against the current Amazon packaging page (amazon-packaging.com could not be reached in this pass) [S12][S14]. Amazon also publishes form-factor guidelines (2024) that set minimum and maximum dimensions and weights for each tier; packages below the SIOC minimum size can qualify only for PFP [S12][S13].

**"SPS" — VERIFY.** No Amazon tier called "SPS" was found in the sources consulted on 2026-11-09. If a customer document uses "SPS", ask which programme it means (possibly SIPP).

**Other 2025–2026 points**
- Hazmat items cannot be FFP or SIOC certified but can be PFP certified [S12].
- If an ASIN loses certification, Amazon waives the related chargeback for 60 days [S12].
- **ISTA 6-Amazon.com-Over Boxing 2026 (PFP).** ISTA revised the Over Boxing project in March 2026: air pillows removed and kraft paper dunnage (max 12 in [305 mm] wide, 30–50 lb basis weight, manually dispensed) made the only allowed dunnage; a formula for dunnage quantity; updated hazard (dummy) package sizes and weights and over-box sizes; more multi-item configurations and a larger sample size. Test intensities and durations did not change [S38][S39][S40]. Amazon gave a four-month grace period with a cut-off of **1 August 2026**, after which new PFP enrolments must be tested to the 2026 version [S14][S39]. The scope is a retail packaged-product of 32 kg (70 lb) or less placed by Amazon into an over-box [S1][S38]. Top load has not been a required part of the Over Boxing vibration block since April 2018 [S40].
- Chargeback and packaging-fee amounts for uncertified items vary by source and changed in 2025–2026. **VERIFY** current figures in Amazon Vendor Central before quoting them.

**ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC test types.** The 2018 edition (still the current overview on ista.org) defines eight types set by Amazon outbound shipment method, fulfilment-centre handling method, packaged-product weight and product category [S10]. Girth here means length + 2 × (width + height) [S10].

| Type | Outbound shipment | FC handling | Weight / size |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Parcel, individual packaged-product | Standard (floor loaded) | < 50 lb (23 kg) and girth ≤ 165 in (4.19 m) [S9] |
| B | Parcel | Standard | 50 lb (23 kg) to < 100 lb (45 kg), girth ≤ 165 in |
| C | Parcel | Standard | ≥ 100 lb (45 kg), girth ≤ 165 in |
| D | LTL, individual packaged-product | Standard | < 100 lb (45 kg) or girth > 165 in |
| E | LTL | Standard | ≥ 100 lb (45 kg) or girth > 165 in |
| F | LTL | Pallet handling (received on its own pallet) | no weight or size limit |
| G | Parcel — TV/monitor | Standard | < 150 lb (68 kg), girth ≤ 165 in |
| H | LTL — TV/monitor | Standard | ≥ 150 lb (68 kg) or girth > 165 in |

Types G and H (TVs and monitors) were added in March 2018, together with a compression-machine alternative for clamp testing and clamp-before-stack ordering [S40]. Fragile items need five samples; non-fragile items need one (TVs and monitors count as non-fragile) [S10]. Type A follows a 3A-like sequence: 12-hour ambient preconditioning, optional conditioning, 9 drops, random vibration (0.53 Grms overall level is the first stated), 8 drops, then rotational, bridge and concentrated impacts for the relevant package shapes and a leak test for liquids [S10]. Heavier types add elements such as tip/tip-over (22° tip angle for tall packages), clamp handling and vertical compression [S10][S40]. The per-type element list and heights are in the purchased procedure.

## ASTM D4169: distribution cycles

ASTM D4169 (current edition D4169-23e1, listed Active on the ASTM store and last updated 27 March 2024; no later edition found on 2026-11-09) is a standard practice for performance testing of shipping containers and systems [S15][S16][S43]. It defines 18 distribution cycles, 10 test schedules (A–J) and 3 assurance levels [S18]. Distribution cycles are defined by transport mode and load type, not by product type [S16].

Distribution cycles confirmed by more than one secondary source:

| DC | Description |
|---|---|
| DC 1 | General cycle — undefined distribution system [S16][S17] |
| DC 2 | User-specified cycle — route and hazards well defined by the user [S17] |
| DC 3 | Single package, not unitised, less-than-truckload (LTL) motor freight [S16][S17] |
| DC 12 | Air and motor freight, over 150 lb (68 kg) or unitised [S17] |
| DC 13 | Air and motor freight (small parcel), single package 150 lb (68 kg) or less — the most common choice for medical devices [S16][S17] |
| DC 18 | Non-commercial government shipments, protection levels from MIL-STD-2073-1 [S19] |

Cycles seen in a single secondary source only (keep as **VERIFY** until checked against Table 1):

| DC | Description reported |
|---|---|
| DC 6 | Motor freight, truckload or LTL, unitised (palletised) [S44] |
| DC 7 | Rail only, bulk loaded [S44] |
| DC 8 | Rail only, unitised [S44] |

Other cycles (DC 4, DC 5, DC 9 to DC 11, DC 14 to DC 17) cover truckload, rail, rail-plus-motor, air, export and other combinations. Lab summaries group them loosely (one groups DC 7–11 as rail) and disagree on details: one describes DC 12 as air and motor freight for heavy or unitised loads, another as motor freight only, and one calls DC 2 an LTL cycle although DC 2 is the user-specified cycle [S17][S44][S45]. **VERIFY** every cycle against Table 1 of D4169-23e1 before writing a protocol. Lab guidance also differs on the default assurance level for sterile medical devices (AL II per the standard's general recommendation; some labs advise AL I) [S44].

## ASTM D4169: assurance levels and test schedules

**Assurance levels** set test intensity by probability of occurrence [S16][S19]:
- **Level I** — highest intensity, lowest probability of occurrence.
- **Level II** — intermediate; the usual default unless conditions dictate otherwise.
- **Level III** — lowest intensity, highest probability of occurrence.

**Test schedules (hazard elements)** confirmed by sources [S16][S18]:

| Schedule | Hazard |
|---|---|
| A | Handling — manual and mechanical (drops, impacts, stability) |
| B | Warehouse stacking (compression) |
| C | Vehicle stacking (compression) |
| D | VERIFY (commonly reported as stacked vibration) |
| E | Vehicle vibration (random) |
| F | VERIFY (commonly reported as loose-load vibration) |
| G | VERIFY (commonly reported as rail switching impact) |
| H | Environmental hazard (temperature/humidity) |
| I | Low pressure (altitude) hazard |
| J | Concentrated impact |

Secondary sources report that D4169-23 changed vehicle stacking top-load values, and D4169-23e1 changed compression testing, the definition of small and lightweight packages, and when to use stack-height values [S15]. **VERIFY** in the standard.

## ASTM D4169: drop heights versus weight and handling

Drop height in Schedule A depends on **package weight**, **assurance level** and **handling type** (manual handling of single packages vs mechanical handling of unitised loads). Heavier packages are dropped from lower heights, because they are less likely to be thrown or carried high.

Assurance Level I example for manual handling of cartons [S20]:

| Package weight | AL I drop height |
|---|---|
| < 20 lb (< 9.1 kg) | 24 in (610 mm) |
| 20–40 lb (9.1–18.1 kg) | 21 in (533 mm) |
| 40–60 lb (18.1–27.2 kg) | 18 in (457 mm) |
| 60–80 lb (27.2–36.3 kg) | 15 in (381 mm) |

## Box compression testing: ASTM D642 / ISO 12048 / TAPPI T 804

**What it is.** A compression tester moves one platen at a constant rate and records the force–deflection curve until the container fails; the peak force is the box compression test (BCT) value. Tests are run on conditioned containers (see conditioning below).

- **ASTM D642** (D642-20) measures compressive resistance of shipping containers, components and unit loads, allows fixed or swivel platens, and is recognised as fulfilling ISO 12048 [S32].
- **ISO 12048** is the ISO compression and stacking test using a compression tester [S32].
- **TAPPI T 804** (om-24) tests corrugated or solid fibre containers top-to-bottom, end-to-end or side-to-side; it does not recognise swivel-platen machines [S32][S33].
- For sustained (dead) loads, ASTM D4577 is the constant-load companion method [S32].

Fixed-platen BCT is normally higher than swivel-platen BCT for the same box. Record the platen type and test speed with every result.

## Converting test BCT to allowable stacking load

A laboratory BCT is a short-term, standard-atmosphere value. In service the box is weakened by time under load, humidity, pallet pattern and handling. The usual method multiplies the BCT by environmental factors, or equivalently divides the required load by the combined factor [S36].

```
L_top   = (n - 1) × m_box × g
F_comb  = f_time × f_RH × f_pattern × f_overhang × f_other
BCT_req = L_top / F_comb          (safety factor SF = 1 / F_comb)
```

Variables:
- `L_top` — load on the bottom box from the boxes above it, N [lbf]
- `n` — number of boxes in the column (tiers), –
- `m_box` — gross mass of one filled box, kg [lb]
- `g` — 9.80665 m/s² (in US units, mass in lb gives load in lbf directly)
- `f_…` — environmental reduction factors, each ≤ 1, dimensionless
- `BCT_req` — minimum laboratory BCT needed, N [lbf]

Published multipliers (Fibre Box Association data reported by a converter) [S34]:

| Condition | Multiplier | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 10 days under load | 0.63 | |
| 90 days under load | 0.55 | |
| Interlocked stacking | 0.40–0.60 | 0.60 best case, 0.40 worst case |
| Pallet overhang | 0.60–0.80 | another source gives a 30–50 % loss (0.50–0.70) — **VERIFY** [S35]; a third gives 20–40 % [S37] |
| 90 % RH | about 0.50 | "roughly half or more" of dry strength lost [S35]; **VERIFY** against the FBA chart |

Validity: corrugated boxes in columnar stacking where the box walls carry the load. Mixed boxes in a column: use the lowest BCT [S36]. These multipliers are industry-typical — *replace with supplier/house data in `company/`*. See `performance/` for McKee BCT estimates and full environmental-factor tables.

**Worked example (SI).** A pallet column of 10 boxes, 15 kg each, stored 90 days with pallet overhang. Load on the bottom box `L_top = 9 × 15 × 9.80665 = 1 323.9 N`. Combined factor `F_comb = 0.55 × 0.80 = 0.44`. Required BCT `= 1 323.9 / 0.44 = 3 009 N` (safety factor 2.27).

**Worked example (US).** Same column: 9 × 33.07 lb = 297.6 lbf on the bottom box. `BCT_req = 297.6 / 0.44 = 676 lbf`.

Limitations: multipliers are not truly independent, so multiplying many of them can be over-conservative; BCT is a static laboratory value and does not reproduce dynamic transport loads [S36]; there is no single standard safety factor — use customer specifications or your own risk assessment [S36].

## Random vibration: PSD profiles, truck/air/rail and Grms

**Method vs profile.** ASTM D4728 is the random-vibration method; it cannot be used alone and must be combined with a requirement giving at least the PSD, test duration and overall Grms [S24]. The profile normally comes from ASTM D4169 Schedule E, an ISTA procedure, or field measurement.

**PSD and Grms.** A power spectral density (PSD) profile gives acceleration intensity per frequency band, in g²/Hz, over a frequency range. The overall intensity is the root-mean-square acceleration:

```
Grms = sqrt( ∫ PSD(f) df )      (integral over the test frequency range)
```

Variables: `PSD(f)` in g²/Hz; `f` in Hz; `Grms` in g (1 g = 9.80665 m/s² [386.09 in/s²]). Valid for stationary Gaussian random vibration. For a segmented PSD with sloped lines on log–log axes, integrate each segment using its slope (dB/octave); the flat-rectangle shortcut below only applies to flat segments.

**Worked example.** A flat PSD of 0.01 g²/Hz from 1 Hz to 200 Hz: area = 0.01 × 199 = 1.99 g², so `Grms = sqrt(1.99) = 1.41 g` (13.8 m/s² rms [544 in/s² rms]). Grms is the same in SI and US because it is expressed in g.

**Published profile levels.**
- ASTM D4169 truck, Assurance Level II: 0.52 Grms, comparable to the 95th percentile field intensity of 0.50 Grms in the FPL-22 data used for the original profile [S22]. ASTM has since revised the truck profiles into a set of three (low, medium, high) with similar shapes and different Grms levels, run in sequence [S23]. **VERIFY** current low/medium/high Grms levels and their durations per assurance level in D4169-23e1; no source quoting them was reachable in this pass.
- ISTA 3A (2018 edition) and ISTA 6-Amazon.com-SIOC Type A: overall levels 0.53 Grms and 0.46 Grms for the with- and without-top-load blocks [S3][S10]; ISTA 3B (2017) and 3E (2026): 0.54 Grms [S8][S41]; ISTA 3K (2011) mixed load: 0.28 Grms [S42].
- Air and rail profiles exist in D4169 Schedule E; their Grms values were not confirmed in this pass — **VERIFY**.

Limitations: Grms alone does not describe a profile; two PSDs with equal Grms can damage a product differently if the energy is near a product resonance. Always compare the PSD shape with the product's and the cushion system's natural frequencies (see `cushioning/cushion-design.md`).

## Test conditioning: TAPPI T 402, ISO 187, ASTM D4332

**Paper and board (material tests and container compression).**
- **TAPPI T 402 / ASTM D685** (technically equivalent): precondition at 10–35 % RH and 22–40 °C [72–104 °F], then condition and test at 23.0 ± 1.0 °C [73.4 ± 1.8 °F] and 50.0 ± 2.0 % RH [S25]. Preconditioning from the dry side means the sample approaches equilibrium by absorbing moisture, which reduces hysteresis scatter. T 402 does not cover special tropical or arctic atmospheres [S25].
- **ISO 187** (current edition 2022): standard atmosphere 23 °C / 50 % RH; 27 °C / 65 % RH is permitted in tropical countries [S26]. The 1990 edition gave tolerances of ± 1 °C and ± 2 % RH for both [S27]. India's adoption uses 27 ± 2 °C and 65 ± 5 % RH [S28].

**Complete packages.**
- **ASTM D4332** (current edition D4332-22 [S46]) gives standard and special atmospheres. Values reported consistently by several test laboratories [S29][S31][S47]:
  - standard: 23 ± 1 °C [73.4 ± 1.8 °F], 50 ± 2 % RH, normally at least 72 h; preconditioning at 20–40 °C, 10–35 % RH;
  - frozen: −18 ± 2 °C [0 ± 4 °F], humidity uncontrolled;
  - tropical: 40 ± 2 °C [104 ± 4 °F], 90 ± 5 % RH;
  - desert: 60 ± 2 °C [140 ± 4 °F], 15 ± 5 % RH (the standard warns that constant 60 °C can affect some materials in ways cyclic field exposure would not).
  Durations at special conditions vary by laboratory (24–72 h, sometimes longer); the specifying document sets them. For box compression at standard atmosphere, D4332 points to D685 (= TAPPI T 402) conditioning [S46].
- ISTA 2A/3A list a hot-humid condition of 38 °C [100 °F] at 85 % RH [S30]. An isolated secondary mention of a D4332 "tropical" 38 °C / 90 % RH condition [S31] appears to mix this ISTA condition with D4332; the D4332 tropical value used here is 40 °C / 90 % RH.

Corrugated board loses compression strength as moisture content rises, so a box tested after tropical conditioning will show a much lower BCT than one tested at 23 °C / 50 % RH. Always state the conditioning atmosphere and time with any test result.

## Practical test-planning checklist

1. Identify the distribution channel (parcel, LTL, truckload, DC-to-store, Amazon) and any customer-mandated procedure.
2. Choose the procedure: ISTA (named by customer or 3-Series by channel) or ASTM D4169 (DC + AL).
3. Define pass/fail criteria for product and package before testing.
4. Choose conditioning (standard 23 °C / 50 % RH, plus climate extremes if the route requires).
5. Run in an ISTA-certified or accredited laboratory when certification (e.g. Amazon) is required.
6. Record procedure edition, platen type, PSD/Grms, drop heights, conditioning and sample count in the report.
