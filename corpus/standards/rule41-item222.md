# NMFC Item 222 and UFC Rule 41

> **Normative text:** this is a summary for orientation. The governing text is the current NMFC
> (published by NMFTA) and the Uniform Freight Classification (UFC 6000 series). Do not certify a
> box from this file — check the current tariff.
>
> **Source access note (2026-11-09, wave 1 update):** the official NMFC/UFC texts are paid
> publications and the NMFTA and Railinc sites could not be reached from the research session.
> The grade tables below now come from an Item 222-derived chart in a retailer specification hosted
> by the Fibre Box Association [S17], which was read in full. It agrees with converter charts
> [S3][S4][S6]. Cells found only in converter charts are still marked **VERIFY**.

## What the rules are

- **UFC Rule 41** sets requirements for corrugated and solid fibreboard boxes in rail shipment;
  **NMFC Item 222** is the similar, but not identical, rule for common-carrier motor (LTL)
  freight [S1].
- Both specify board grade by the box's **gross weight** (box + contents) and **size**
  (united inches = outside L + W + D), and require a **box maker's certificate (BMC)** on
  conforming boxes [S2][S7][S15].
- Two alternative board tables: Table A — burst (Mullen) test **plus** minimum combined weight of
  facings; Table B — minimum edge crush test (ECT) [S2]. The facings-weight requirement does **not**
  apply to ECT-rated board [S2][S8].
- A box must satisfy both the weight limit and the size limit of its row; a large light box may
  need a higher grade than its weight alone suggests [S7].
- The rules are material specifications, not performance tests: there is no way to correlate them
  with ISTA pre-shipment tests [S1]. ASTM D5639 offers a performance-based selection practice and
  requires BMC values, where printed, to meet the minimums for the packaged size and weight [S14].

## Compliance check — worked example (derived)

Box 400 × 300 × 300 mm outside, gross mass 12 kg.
- United inches: (400 + 300 + 300) / 25.4 = **39.37 in** (1000 mm) — fits the 40 in row.
- Gross weight: 12 / 0.45359 = **26.46 lb** — exceeds the 20 lb limit of the 23 ECT row.
- Lowest single-wall row meeting both: **35 lb / 50 in → 26 ECT (4.55 kN/m)** [S3], or 150 psi
  burst with 66 lb/MSF facings [S19]. These light-duty rows come from secondary charts only
  (VERIFY). If you use only the rows confirmed by [S17], the lowest is **65 lb / 75 in → 32 ECT
  (5.60 kN/m) or 200 psi + 84 lb/MSF** [S17].
- Carrier rule compliance is a minimum: stacking strength must still be checked separately
  (`kb.performance.stacking-load-calc`).

Limitations: tables are gross-weight/size thresholds only; they say nothing about stacking,
humidity or transit performance [S1].

## Box maker's certificate (BMC) — stamp fields

A BMC is a printed seal, usually on the bottom of the box, stating the construction and rated
limits [S20]. The certificate is optional, but using it implies the box complies with the rules it
refers to [S20]. Fields, as shown on the sample single-wall (ECT) and double-wall (burst) seals in
[S17] [S13][S2]:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Certifying statement | Says the box meets all construction requirements of the applicable freight classification [S17] (paraphrased; see the NMFC for exact wording) |
| Board construction | Single wall, double wall, triple wall (or solid fibre) |
| Test rating | **Either** Bursting test (psi) **with** Min. combined weight of facings (lb/MSF), **or** Edge crush test (ECT, lbf/in) — an ECT certificate does not carry the facings weight [S2] |
| Size limit | Maximum united inches (outside L + W + D) |
| Gross weight limit | Maximum weight of box plus contents (lb) |
| Box manufacturer | Name of the box maker, with city and state [S17] |

- The test stated on the certificate must not be less than the minimum for the gross weight and
  size limit [S13].
- Loading to the gross weight limit does not guarantee damage-free delivery [S13].
- A 2009 NMFC docket amended Item 222-1 (certificate of box manufacturer) for clarification [S16].

## Current regulatory status (as of 2026-11-09)

- **Item 222 still exists.** NMFTA's Docket 2026-2 Subject 32 analysis still refers to boxes "as
  described in Items 220, 221 and 222" [S9]. No docket cancelling or replacing Item 222 was found
  in the sources consulted (**VERIFY** against the current NMFC before relying on this).
- Docket 2026-2 Subject 32 proposes cancelling **Item 685** (packing or packaging — alternate
  forms), not Item 222 [S9]. Status of that proposal: **VERIFY**.
- **Docket 2026-2 (Fall 2026)** had a public meeting on 29 September 2026. Its subject list runs
  from Subject 1 to Subject 33 [S21][S22]. The packaging-related subjects listed are:
  - Subject 30: Item 245, definition of or specifications for crates;
  - Subject 32: Item 685, packing or packaging, alternate forms. NMFTA's docket page links both an
    analysis and a disposition for Subject 32 [S21].

  No Item 222 subject appears in the list [S21]. These details come from search-engine extracts of
  the NMFTA pages, because nmfta.org was blocked in the research session. The text of the
  Subject 32 disposition and its effective date were not read. NMFTA says it now posts only the
  most current docket documents publicly [S21].
- **2025 overhaul was about classification, not box construction:** Docket 2025-1 (approved as
  modified in Disposition Bulletin 1371) moved many commodities from commodity-based to
  density-based classes, effective 19 July 2025, reclassifying or consolidating over 2,000 NMFC
  items and expanding the density scale [S10][S12]. Docket 2025-2 followed (effective
  6 December 2025 per secondary sources) [S11]. Engineers should expect density (and therefore box
  cube) to drive freight cost more than before.
- Earlier dockets touching Item 222: a 2022-2 docket proposal related to Item 222 fibreboard
  packages (effective 13 August 2022) [S15]. Content of that change: **VERIFY**.
- **UFC Rule 41** remains in the rail Uniform Freight Classification; Railinc's UFC 6000-M
  Supplement 8 still indexes Rule 41 for fibre-box requirements [S8]. Effective dates of the
  latest supplement: **VERIFY**.
- Many shippers no longer rely on Item 222 alone: retailers (e.g. [S17]) and parcel carriers
  publish their own minimums, and performance testing (ISTA / ASTM D4169, ASTM D5639) is used
  instead of material rules [S1][S14].
