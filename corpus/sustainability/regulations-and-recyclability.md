# Packaging regulations, recyclability and sustainability claims

Regulatory status changes quickly. Everything here reflects sources consulted on **2026-11-09**. The EU PPWR sections were checked against the Official Journal text on EUR-Lex [S18]. The FBA, Cepi/4evergreen, FEFCO and FBA LCA sections were read from the publishers' own PDFs. State agency, ISO, FSC/PEFC/SFI and FTC sites could not be opened from this session, so those items rest on search-engine summaries of the agency or scheme pages, and are flagged where they still need checking. For anything normative, point engineers to the legal text (EUR-Lex, state statute or rule) and to company legal or compliance staff. Do not give legal advice.

## EU PPWR — Regulation (EU) 2025/40: status and key dates

- Published in the Official Journal on 22 January 2025, in force 11 February 2025 (twentieth day after publication), and **applies from 12 August 2026**; Article 67(5) applies from 12 February 2029 [S18][S1].
- Many obligations run on their own dates. Several are "the later of a fixed date or N months/years after a Commission delegated or implementing act" [S18]. Secondary commentaries consulted in the previous wave agree with these dates [S2][S3][S5][S6][S7][S8][S9][S16].
- Amendments: as of 2026-11-09 EUR-Lex lists corrigenda (language versions only) and two amendment **proposals** that do not touch the design articles summarised here: COM(2025) 982 (suspending the authorised-representative rule for EPR) and COM(2026) 590 (public procurement). Annulment actions are pending before the General Court against parts of Art. 29 (including the cardboard-box exemption in Art. 29(4)(d)) and Art. 50 [S19]. Watch these before relying on Art. 29.

## EU PPWR — recyclability performance grades

Article 6 requires all packaging placed on the market to be recyclable: designed for material recycling and, from 2035, also collected, sorted and recycled at scale [S18]. Recyclability is expressed as grade A, B or C under Table 3 of Annex II. The grade comes from a weighted assessment of the packaging unit against design-for-recycling criteria that the Commission will set by delegated act per packaging category and predominant material [S18]:

| Grade | Assessment per unit, in terms of weighting (Annex II Table 3) | Market access |
|---|---|---|
| A | ≥ 95 % | Allowed |
| B | ≥ 80 % | Allowed |
| C | ≥ 70 % | Allowed from the 2030 date; not allowed from 1 Jan 2038 |
| Below C | < 70 % | "Technically non-recyclable": not allowed from the 2030 date |

Confirmed against Annex II Table 3 [S18]. Further points from the legal text [S18]:

- Until the delegated acts exist (due 1 Jan 2028), no official A/B/C grade can be calculated [S18][S4].
- Paper/cardboard (except liquid packaging board) is its own category in Annex II Table 2 [S18].
- "Recycled at scale" (from 2035) means at least **55 %** of the category recycled annually at EU level for materials other than wood (30 % for wood) (Art. 3(1)(39)) [S18].
- Integrated components (for example glued-on labels or windows) are assessed with the unit. Separate components are assessed separately, and no component may hinder recycling of the main body (Art. 6(9)) [S18].
- Economic operators have 3 years to comply with new or updated design-for-recycling criteria (Art. 6(4)) [S18].
- EPR fees are to be modulated by grade 18 months after the acts enter into force (Art. 6(8)) [S18].
- Industry test protocols (Cepi method, 4evergreen evaluation, see below) are the likely evidence base, but the delegated act decides.

## EU PPWR — recycled content (Article 7)

Article 7's minimum recycled-content targets apply only to the **plastic part** of packaging (for example 35 % from the 2030 date for plastic packaging not listed elsewhere, with higher 2040 targets) [S18]. All-fibre corrugated and paperboard packaging has no PPWR recycled-content mandate. A plastic window, film laminate or plastic component on a paper pack is a plastic part and is in scope. Check Art. 7 for the exemptions and the 2040 figures before quoting them.

## EU PPWR — empty space ratio (Article 24) and minimisation (Article 10)

- **Grouped, transport and e-commerce packaging (Art. 24(1))**: economic operators who *fill* it must keep the empty space ratio at or below **50 %**, from the later of 1 January 2030 or 3 years after the method implementing act [S18].
- **Definitions (Art. 24(3))**: empty space is the total volume of the outer (grouped, transport or e-commerce) pack minus the volume of the **sales packaging** inside. The ratio is that empty space divided by the total volume. Filling materials (paper, air cushions, bubble wrap, foam, wood wool, polystyrene chips) count as empty space [S18].
- **Method still to come (Art. 24(2))**: the implementing act (due 12 Feb 2028) must allow for irregular shapes, multiple items, liquids, fragile or easily damaged products, small products at risk from larger ones, and room for shipping labels [S18].
- **Sales packaging (Art. 24(4))**: from 12 February 2028, fillers of sales packaging must reduce empty space to the minimum needed for function, including product protection. For products that settle or need headspace, compliance is judged at filling, and air or protective gas within food does not count [S18].
- **Exemptions (Art. 24(5))**: operators using sales packaging as e-commerce packaging, and operators using reusable packaging in a re-use system, are exempt from the 50 % rule. Such sales packaging must still meet Art. 10 [S18].
- **Review (Art. 24(6))**: by 12 Feb 2032 the Commission reviews the ratio and exemptions, including possible ratios for sales packaging of toys, cosmetics, DIY kits and electronics [S18].
- **Minimisation (Art. 10)**: by 1 January 2030, weight and volume must be the minimum necessary for function, judged against the Annex IV performance criteria. Features that only enlarge perceived volume (double walls, false bottoms, unnecessary layers) are banned unless covered by the design-right/trademark (protected before 11 Feb 2025) or geographical-indication exceptions. The technical documentation must show which design requirements prevent further reduction [S18].

### Empty space ratio working formula (pending the official method)

```
ESR = (V_pack − V_contents) / V_pack × 100 %
```

- `V_pack`: total volume of the grouped, transport or e-commerce packaging (L) [in³] [S18]. Whether this is the inside or outside volume is for the implementing act to settle. Until then, state which basis you used (VERIFY when the act is adopted).
- `V_contents`: volume of the sales packaging contained (L) [in³] [S18]. Use the bare product volume only where the product ships without sales packaging. Filling material counts as empty space, so it is **not** included [S18].

Worked example, SI: box inside 400 × 300 × 200 mm (24.0 L) holding one sales pack of 300 × 200 × 120 mm (7.2 L): ESR = (24.0 − 7.2)/24.0 = **70.0 %**, above the limit. Right-sized to 310 × 210 × 130 mm (8.463 L): ESR = **14.9 %**.
Worked example, US: box inside 16 × 12 × 8 in (1536 in³ = 25.17 L) holding a 12 × 8 × 5 in sales pack (480 in³): ESR = **68.75 %**. Right-sized to 12.5 × 8.5 × 5.5 in (584.4 in³): ESR = **17.9 %**.

Limits: this is a working definition that follows the Art. 24(3) wording [S18]. Irregular products, multi-item packs and the inside-versus-outside basis await the implementing act. Derived.

## EU PPWR — reuse targets (Article 29)

- **Transport packaging (Art. 29(1))**: from 1 January 2030, operators using transport packaging, or sales packaging used for transport (including e-commerce), within the EU must have at least **40 %** of it reusable within a re-use system. The forms covered are pallets, foldable plastic boxes, boxes, trays, plastic crates, IBCs, pails, drums and canisters, including flexible formats and pallet wrapping or straps. From 1 January 2040 operators "shall endeavour" to reach **70 %** [S18]. Pallet wrap and straps are therefore *in* scope; an earlier draft of this file said they were exempt, which is wrong.
- **Own sites and same-country B2B (Art. 29(2)–(3))**: from 2030, transport packaging moved between an operator's own sites (or linked/partner enterprises), or delivered to another operator in the same Member State, must be reusable in a re-use system [S18].
- **Exclusions (Art. 29(4))**: paragraphs 1–3 do not apply to packaging for dangerous goods, custom-designed packaging for large machinery, equipment and commodities, flexible formats in direct food or feed contact, or packaging **in the form of cardboard boxes** [S18]. The cardboard exclusion is confirmed in the legal text. A pending annulment action targets Art. 29(4)(d) [S19], so watch it.
- **Grouped packaging (Art. 29(5))**: from 2030, at least **10 %** of grouped packaging in the form of boxes, **excluding cardboard**, must be reusable; 25 % is the 2040 endeavour [S18].
- **Beverages (Art. 29(6))**: 10 % in reusable packaging from 2030 (40 % endeavour by 2040), with listed exclusions [S18].
- **Micro-enterprises (Art. 29(13))**: exempt for a year in which they place no more than 1000 kg of packaging on a Member State market and meet the micro-enterprise definition. Member States may also grant 5-year exemptions under conditions (Art. 29(14)) [S18].

## EU PPWR — labelling (Article 12)

- **Harmonised sorting label (Art. 12(1))**: from the later of 12 August 2028 or 24 months after the implementing act, packaging must carry a pictogram label on material composition. **Transport packaging is outside this duty, except e-commerce packaging**, as is packaging in a deposit-return system [S18]. A plain corrugated transport case therefore needs no harmonised label. An e-commerce shipper does. The implementing act was due 12 August 2026 [S18]; trade sources say that deadline was missed [S10][S11] (VERIFY on EUR-Lex).
- **Recycled-content labels (Art. 12(4))**: any voluntary recycled-content label on packaging covered by Art. 7 must follow the implementing act's format and the Art. 7(8) method [S18].
- **Reusable packaging label (Art. 12(2))**: from the later of 12 February 2029 or 30 months after the act [S18].
- **EPR mark (Art. 12(9))**: by 12 February 2027, EPR membership may be shown only by a symbol in a QR code or other open digital marking, which must not mislead about recyclability [S18].
- **Misleading marks (Art. 12(8))**: labels likely to confuse end users about the harmonised features are prohibited [S18].
- **Sell-off (Art. 12(12))**: packaging made or imported before a labelling deadline may be sold for 3 years after that requirement applies [S18].
- National schemes (for example France's Triman) stay in force meanwhile. Trade advice is not to commission harmonised-label artwork until the act is adopted [S11].

## Repulpability, wax and water-resistant coatings (FBA Voluntary Standard)

Wax-impregnated or wax-cascaded corrugated is traditionally excluded from standard old corrugated container (OCC) recycling. The US route back into OCC for treated board is the **AF&PA–FBA Voluntary Standard for Repulping and Recycling Coated or Treated Corrugated Fiberboard and its Equivalents in the OCC Recycling Stream**, revised 31 March 2025. It replaces the 2013 standard for new applications, and treatments registered under earlier versions are grandfathered [S20]. Test protocol summary [S20]:

- **Part 1, repulpability**: 100 % treated material is blended and disintegrated in water at 52 °C ± 3 °C (125 °F ± 5 °F), pH 7 ± 0.5, then screened on a 0.25 mm (0.010 in) slotted flat screen.
  - Pass: fibre yield **≥ 60 %** of the oven-dry charge.
  - Pass: rejects **≤ 20 %** of accepts plus rejects.
- **Part 2, recyclability**: a pilot-pulper run of **20 % treated and 80 % untreated** corrugated is compared with a 100 % untreated control. Each passes through a pressure screen with 1.57 mm (0.062 in) holes or 0.61 mm (0.024 in) slots, then a 0.25 mm slotted screen and a lightweight cleaner. 3.0 g handsheets are made from the cleaner accepts. Pass criteria compare the test sheets with the control:
  - Appearance: no substantial difference.
  - Stickies (area ≥ 0.4 mm²): **≤ 15**, or no more than 30 % above the control (average of 3 sheets).
  - Slide angle: drop **≤ 15 %**.
  - STFI short-span compression and burst, normalised to basis weight: drop **≤ 10 %**.
  - Water-drop penetration: no more than **200 s** longer than the control.
- **Operational criteria**: no acid wash or screen dismantling needed to finish, and no visible deposits in blender, disintegrator or pulper.
- **Certification**: up to three trials, and the sample must pass all tests in two. Testing is done by an FBA-approved lab; manufacturers may not self-certify. Recertify after a significant change in treatment, substrate chemistry (for example adding wet-strength resin) or a higher treatment-to-fibre ratio. Conventional wet-strength containerboard is not considered repulpable unless it has passed.
- **Marking**: registered products may use the modified Corrugated Recycles symbol, shown with the box maker's name and location.

Other coatings: barrier coatings, plastic laminates, heavy UV varnishes, metallised films, wet-strength resins and hot-melt adhesives can all cut repulping yield or add stickies. Ask the coating supplier for test data under a named protocol: FBA in the US [S20], or the Cepi method with the 4evergreen evaluation in Europe [S21][S22].

Water-based inkjet prints: a German test body (PTS) reported that HP water-based digital prints introduced no optically disturbing inhomogeneities in recycled pulp [S17].

## Recyclability protocols and standards

Name the protocol when making any recyclability statement.

- **Cepi harmonised laboratory test method, Part I (Version 3, February 2025)**: emulates a conventional recycling mill without flotation deinking [S21]. It is intended to supplement the EN 13430 recyclability evaluation and to support third-party grading systems [S21]. Procedure:
  - Disintegrate 50 g dry sample at 2.5 % consistency in tap water at 40 °C, pH 7–8, for 10 min (30 000 rev), with no pre-soaking. Extend to 20 min if total screening reject (CR + 0.9 FR) is ≥ 15 % and fibre is in the rejects.
  - Measure filtrate evaporation residue (COD and BOD optional).
  - Measure coarse reject on a 5 mm hole plate and fine reject on a 150 µm slot plate (Somerville fractionator).
  - Run sheet adhesion and visual impurity tests on the accepts.
  - Characterise the rejects. Macrostickies are optional.
  - Samples with wet-strength agents younger than 30 days are aged artificially first [S21][S22].
- **4evergreen evaluation protocol**: turns the Cepi method's measured parameters into a recyclability evaluation and score for fibre-based packaging [S23][S22]. The January 2025 annexes add, for example, a decision tree for **two-side barrier-coated** samples: if neither barrier fragments during repulping, the evaluation protocol cannot be applied [S22]. Cepi lists the laboratories verified to run the method (for example PTS, CTP, KCL, Innovhub, Lucense, Celabor; list of 18 Aug 2025) [S23]. The current evaluation score thresholds were not in the documents read (VERIFY in the 4evergreen protocol itself).
- **ISO 18601 to ISO 18606 (Packaging and the environment)**:
  - ISO 18601: general requirements for using the series.
  - ISO 18602: optimisation of the packaging system (source reduction).
  - ISO 18603: reuse.
  - ISO 18604: material recycling.
  - ISO 18605: energy recovery.
  - ISO 18606: organic recycling.
  - ISO 18604:2013 (edition 1) is listed as current and "confirmed" at its last systematic review. It sets requirements for packaging to be classed as recoverable by material recycling, applied through the ISO 18601 procedure [S24].
  - ISO/TR 17098:2013 lists substances and materials that may impede recycling [S24].
  - The other parts are 2013 editions per the same catalogue. Check for revisions on iso.org (VERIFY).
- **EN 13432 (packaging recoverable through composting and biodegradation)**: pass criteria as summarised by test bodies and reference sources [S25][S26]:
  - Disintegration: after 12 weeks of composting, at least 90 % passes a 2 mm mesh.
  - Biodegradation: at least 90 % (absolute, or relative to a reference material) within 6 months, measured as CO₂ evolution.
  - Ecotoxicity: compost made with the material must not harm plant germination or biomass.
  - Limits on heavy metals and fluorine.
  - Every constituent of the packaging must comply.
  - EN 13432 is due to be revised [S26]. Quote exact limits from the standard itself; the plant-response threshold and the volatile-solids requirement were not confirmed (VERIFY).

## Forest certification and chain of custody

- **FSC**, **PEFC** and **SFI** certify forest management and, through chain-of-custody (CoC) certification, track certified fibre through every company that takes legal ownership (mill, converter, printer). An on-pack logo needs a valid CoC certificate at each step and trademark approval from the scheme.
- **FSC** (CoC: FSC-STD-40-004; trademarks: FSC-STD-50-001). Recent certificates cite V3-1 and V2-1, and a V3-0 of the trademark standard is referenced in newer guidance. Label claim types [S27]:
  - **FSC 100 %**: all input from FSC-certified forests.
  - **FSC Mix**: under the percentage system, at least **70 %** FSC-certified and/or reclaimed input, with the rest controlled wood. Mix can also be made under the credit system.
  - **FSC Recycled**: all fibre is pre- or post-consumer reclaimed. Paper has no set post-consumer threshold, but all inputs must be verified as reclaimed. Credit and transfer systems are also allowed.
- **PEFC** (CoC: PEFC ST 2002:2020). Label types [S28]:
  - **PEFC Certified**: at least **70 %** PEFC-certified forest-based material.
  - **PEFC Recycled**: for products made of recycled material. Search summaries describe the requirement as "only recycled"; older guidance used a 70 % recycled threshold (VERIFY in PEFC ST 2001).
- **SFI** (SFI 2022 Chain-of-Custody Standard): tracks certified forest content, certified sourcing and recycled content [S29]. On-product labels include a "certified forest content" label for 70–100 % content, recycled-content variants and a certified-sourcing label [S29]. Thresholds were not confirmed (VERIFY in SFI Standards and Rules Section 6).
- The assistant must never state that a product is certified unless the CoC certificate number comes from `company/` records.

## Recycled content and environmental claims (FTC Green Guides)

- **US rule set**: the FTC *Guides for the Use of Environmental Marketing Claims*, 16 CFR Part 260 (last revised 2012). They are interpretive guides on what is deceptive under Section 5 of the FTC Act, not a regulation with fixed thresholds.
- **Revision status**: the FTC opened its regular review with a Federal Register request for comment on 20 December 2022 (comment period extended to April 2023) [S30]. As of 2026-11-09, no final revised Guides were found; trade sources describe the 2012 Guides as still in effect and the update as pending [S30]. The ftc.gov and eCFR sites could not be opened from this session, so confirm on ftc.gov (VERIFY).
- **Recyclable claims (§260.12, 2012 Guides; summary not re-checked against eCFR this wave)**:
  - An unqualified "recyclable" claim is acceptable only if recycling facilities are available to a substantial majority of consumers or communities where the item is sold. The Guides treat 60 % as a substantial majority.
  - Below that, qualify the claim, and qualify it more strongly as access falls.
  - Components that make the item not recyclable must be disclosed.
- **Recycled-content claims (§260.13)**:
  - Only materials recovered or diverted from the waste stream count.
  - Products that are not wholly recycled must state the percentage.
  - Pre-consumer (post-industrial) and post-consumer content may be distinguished, but the claim must be substantiated.
  - The §260.12 and §260.13 summaries come from the 2012 Guides as published and have not been re-checked this wave (VERIFY against eCFR).
- Engineering practice: give recycled content as a percentage by weight with its basis (pre- or post-consumer) and evidence from the mill. Qualify "recyclable" where collection access is limited. Do not make unqualified general claims ("eco-friendly").
- In the EU, PPWR Art. 12(8) forbids marks likely to mislead about the harmonised features, and recycled-content labels must follow the Art. 12(4) format once adopted [S18].

## Design-for-recycling checklist (corrugated and paperboard)

Engineering checklist, derived from the regulatory drivers above. Confirm the material criteria once the PPWR delegated acts are adopted [S18].

1. **Mono-material fibre** where possible. Avoid laminated plastic films, metallised layers and non-separable plastic windows, or make them easy to separate. Integrated components count in the grade [S18], and plastic parts bring in Art. 7 recycled content [S18].
2. **Coatings and wax**: use only treatments with repulpability evidence under the FBA standard [S20] or the Cepi method with 4evergreen evaluation [S21][S22]. Record the test report in `company/`.
3. **Adhesives and tapes**: prefer water-based or repulpable adhesives and paper tape. Minimise hot-melt, which drives stickies, a pass criterion in both protocols [S20][S21].
4. **Inks and varnish**: water-based or low-migration systems. Avoid heavy UV coatings over whole surfaces unless repulpability is shown [S17].
5. **Right-size**: check the empty space ratio (≤ 50 % for transport, grouped and e-commerce packs under Art. 24) and minimise under Art. 10 [S18].
6. **Remove unnecessary components**: double walls, false bottoms and unnecessary layers are banned when they only enlarge perceived volume [S18]. Remove inner sleeves and dunnage that serve no function.
7. **Labelling**: plan space for the EU harmonised label on sales and e-commerce packs once adopted (not needed on non-e-commerce transport packs), and avoid conflicting on-pack claims [S18][S11].
8. **Evidence**: keep a technical file with the recyclability assessment, protocol and lab, material composition, recycled-content evidence and CoC certificate numbers. PPWR Annex VII technical documentation is required for Arts 6 and 10 [S18].

## Right-sizing and void ratio formulas

```
Void ratio      VR  = 1 − V_product / V_inside
Cube efficiency CE  = V_product / V_inside   (= 1 − VR)
Board saving    ΔA% = (A_blank,old − A_blank,new) / A_blank,old × 100 %
```

- `V_product`: volume of the product or its bounding box (L) [in³]. State which.
- `V_inside`: inside volume of the shipper from **inside dimensions** L × W × D (L) [in³].
- `A_blank`: blank area of the box from the blank formulas in `dimensional/` (m²) [ft²].

VR on the inside basis matches the ESR worked example above. For the 400 × 300 × 200 mm box holding 7.2 L, VR = 70.0 %; after right-sizing, 14.9 % (SI). The US example gives 68.75 % → 17.9 %.

Limits:

- Bounding-box volume overstates the fill for irregular products.
- Under PPWR Art. 24 the contents term is the *sales packaging* volume, not the bare product [S18].
- Cushioning needs (see `cushioning/`) set a lower limit on clearance, so do not right-size below the cushion thickness the fragility analysis requires.
