# Manufacturing tolerances, inspection sampling and tolerance stack-up

> Confidence: **industry-typical** unless a line cites a standard. Replace with supplier/house data in `company/`. Customer specifications and `company/` house tolerances override this file.
> Source note: in wave 2 (2026-11-09) the full texts of the FEFCO/VDW Quality Catalogue v4.4 [S1], PMMI B155 TR2.1 and TR2.2 (2018) [S3][S2], FEFCO Recommendation 110 [S11] and the FEFCO Common Footprint specification [S24] were read. ASTM, TAPPI and ISO texts are still extracts or catalogue pages. Check tolerance values in the current edition before you put them in a contract.

## European dimensional tolerances (FEFCO Quality Catalogue)

The FEFCO Quality Catalogue (written by VDW, the German association, version 4.4, August 2014, published by FEFCO) sets size-banded tolerances. They are measured **crease centreline to crease centreline**, or from a crease centreline to the matching outer edge, i.e. on the **score basis**, not the inside [S1]. Dimensional defects are class 2A, AQL 0.65 [S1]:

| Nominal dimension, mm [in] | Tolerance |
|---|---|
| ≤ 300 [≤ 11.8] | ± 3 mm [± 0.118 in] |
| > 300 to 700 [11.8–27.6] | ± 4 mm [± 0.157 in] |
| > 700 to 1200 [27.6–47.2] | ± 0.6 % of the dimension |
| > 1200 [> 47.2] | by agreement |
| Die-cut articles | ± 0.5 %, minimum ± 1 mm [± 0.04 in] |

- Measurement climate: 23 °C, 50 %RH, per EN 20187 (ISO 187) [S1].
- Worked example (SI): 250 mm gives ±3 mm; 500 mm gives ±4 mm; 1000 mm gives ±0.6 % = ±6.0 mm. Die-cut at 150 mm: 0.5 % = 0.75 mm, so the ±1 mm minimum applies. Die-cut at 600 mm gives ±3.0 mm.
- Worked example (US): 20 in (508 mm) is in the 300–700 mm band, so ±4 mm [±0.157 in].
- The ±0.6 % band (> 700 to 1200 mm) is part of the general dimension table (item 3.2-2). The catalogue adds that machine converting may need special tolerances [S1].
- The die-cut tolerance (±0.5 %, min ±1 mm) is item 3.2-3. It has the same tolerance for both processes, but a looser AQL for rotary die-cutting (2B, AQL 4.0) than for flatbed (2B, AQL 1.5) [S1].
- Worked example in the Catalogue: B-flute box, inside 650 × 400 × 250 mm, t = 3 mm, gives crease dimensions 653 × 403 × 256 mm, accepted as 653 ± 4 × 403 ± 4 × 256 ± 3 mm [S1].
- **Other FEFCO/VDW converting tolerances** [S1]: slot position ±2 mm from the longitudinal crease centreline (2A, AQL 1.0); slot depth ±5 mm (2A, AQL 1.0); flap-edge alignment at the manufacturer's joint 0.5 % of the die-cut height, min 3 mm (2B, AQL 1.5); multicolour print register ±2 mm.
- **FEFCO Common Footprint**: theoretical (ordering) size 600 × 400 mm, real (production) size 597 × 398 mm (and likewise for 600 × 200, 500 × 300, 400 × 300 and 300 × 200). Normal industrial tolerance ±1 mm. Height is not specified unless the client asks [S24]. This is a special-purpose tolerance for interchangeable produce trays.

## US tolerances (FBA/PMMI B155, ASTM D2658, customer specifications)

- **Scored and slotted sheets (FBA/PMMI B155 TR2-2)**: an individual panel, measured flat from score line to score line, should not vary by more than ±1/16 in (±1.5 mm). The overall flat sheet should not vary by more than ±1/8 in (±3 mm) [S2].
- **TR2-2 slots and warp**: slot depth within ±1/8 in [±3 mm] of the flap-score centreline; slots centred within 1/16 in [1.5 mm] of the aligning scores. Scope: single and double wall, panels 4–25 in [10.2–63.5 cm], trays at least 1 1/2 in [3.8 cm] deep, for automatic packaging machinery [S2].
- **RSC tolerances (PMMI B155 TR2.1-2018)** [S3]. Scope: top- and end-opening RSCs in **B or C single wall**, burst 150–275 psi or ECT 26–44 lbf/in [4.5–7.7 kN/m], panels 4–25 in [10.2–63.5 cm], for automatic erecting, filling and closing:
  - Panels: ±1/16 in [1.5 mm] score-to-score; overall flat blank ±1/8 in [3 mm].
  - Manufacturer's joint: see the joint section below.
  - Slots: depth ±1/8 in [3 mm] from the flap-score centreline; centred within 1/16 in [1.5 mm] of the aligning scores.
  - Scores: must allow a 180° fold (90° each way) without fracture or continuous checking.
  - **Flap gap**: the major flaps of a closed RSC should not overlap, and the gap between them should not exceed 1/4 in [6.4 mm].
  - Warp: see the warp section below.
  - TR2.1 is a voluntary technical report. It states that it is not a standard and not normative [S3].
- **Customer specification examples** (not industry standards):
  - 3M: ±0.062 in [±1.6 mm] for inside dimensions from 3 to 24 in, and ±0.094 in [±2.4 mm] outside that range, for in-line slotted containers [S5].
  - Snap-on (box-gauge measurement): ±1.6 mm [±1/16 in] for 102–635 mm [4–25 in], and ±3 mm [±1/8 in] for 636–1270 mm [25–50 in] [S6].
- **Common rules of thumb** (supplier guidance, not normative): about ±3 mm for die-cut boxes and about ±5 mm for RSCs [S9]. Practitioners also often quote ±1/8 in [±3 mm] on RSC inside dimensions and ±1/16 in [±1.6 mm] on die-cut boxes. These are **not** box-level FBA values. TR2.1 and TR2.2 set ±1/16 in per panel and ±1/8 in on the overall flat sheet, both measured flat between scores. Neither report sets an inside-dimension tolerance for the erected box [S2][S3]. Turn panel tolerances into inside-size intervals with the stack-up method below.

## Manufacturer's joint: gap, fishtail and score alignment

- **Definition**: ASTM D2658 defines fishtail (skew) as the difference between the gap widths at the top and the bottom, measured at the score lines [S4].
- **PMMI B155 TR2.1-2018, clause 5.1.2 (primary text)** [S3]:
  - Gap at the manufacturer's joint, measured at the flap scorelines: not less than 1/8 in [3 mm] and not more than 1/2 in [12.7 mm].
  - Fishtail (variation in gap width on the same box), measured at the flap scorelines: not more than 3/16 in. The report's soft conversion is 4.5 mm; 3/16 in is exactly 4.76 mm.
  - Gap measured **at the ends of the flaps**: not less than 1/16 in [1.5 mm].
  - Flap-score alignment (corrugator scores at the joint): within 3/16 in.
- AICC cites the same range, with a usual design target of 3/8 in [9.5 mm] [S7].
- **Resolved:** the earlier "1/16 in minimum" from AICC [S8] is not a conflict. TR2.1 applies 1/8 in at the flap scorelines and 1/16 in at the flap ends, where taper narrows the gap [S3].
- **FEFCO/VDW, machine-glued die-cuts** (item 3.2-9) [S1]: normal gap width 6 mm and 8 mm. The catalogue does not say which value goes with which wall type. Tolerance −4/+4 mm on single wall and −4/+6 mm on double wall, measured at the gap edge on the top. AQL 4.0 for rotary and 1.5 for flatbed die-cutting. Manually glued die-cuts need special agreement. The joint must show ≥ 80 % fibre tear (2A, AQL 0.65).
- **Why it matters**: gap and fishtail drive box squareness. A box can still be out of square with a straight gap if the corrugator scores do not line up [S7][S3].

## Caliper, warp and score cracking limits

### Caliper (thickness)

- Caliper lost in converting should be no more than about 0.003 in (0.08 mm) in non-printed areas (industry guidance). Converting crews often crush well past this, which drives mills to specify heavier liners [S12].
- **Target calipers**: a Walmart specification hosted by FBA gives target calipers by grade and flute. Examples: C-flute 200# 0.159 in [4.04 mm]; B-flute 200# 0.121 in [3.07 mm]; BC 200# 0.258 in [6.55 mm]. It allows 0.015 cm less in printed areas [S26]. These are targets, not ± limits.
- The FEFCO/VDW catalogue treats board thickness as approximate and lists flute **heights** (for example B ≥ 2.2 to < 3.1 mm, C ≥ 3.1 to < 4.0 mm). It identifies the flute type by **pitch**, not thickness, because converting changes the thickness [S1].
- National standards that include thickness as a criterion are NF Q 12-008/-009 (France) and SS 84 30 01 (Sweden) [S27]. Their values were not retrieved.
- No published ± caliper acceptance tolerance for combined board was found in a primary source. Set it in house from supplier certificates (**VERIFY**).

### Warp

- The FEFCO/VDW catalogue expresses warp as the maximum rise divided by the span between the supporting edges, as a percentage. Flatness "according to specification" is a 2B defect, AQL 1.5 [S1].
- **FEFCO Recommendation 110 (2018)** replaces the percentage with a **Warp Factor** that does not depend on sheet size: `WF = H × (500 / L)²`. H is the rise and L the subtended chord, both in mm; 500 mm is the reference length. Example from the recommendation: H = 20 mm over L = 500 mm gives WF = 20; the same curve measured as H = 5 mm over L = 250 mm also gives WF = 20 [S11]. (Note: the worked examples in R 110 print the formula as "/ 2", but the figure shows the squared term, and only the squared form gives 20 in both cases. Checked in Python.)
- **PMMI/FBA warp limits** for RSCs and scored-and-slotted sheets, measured as the maximum gap against a straight edge [S2][S3]:

| Span | General limit | High speed (≥ 50 per minute; TR2.2 only) |
|---|---|---|
| 12 in [305 mm] | 1/4 in [6.4 mm] | 3/16 in [4.8 mm] |
| 24 in [610 mm] | 1/2 in [12.7 mm] | 3/8 in [9.5 mm] |
| 36 in [915 mm] | 3/4 in [19.0 mm] | — |
| 48 in [1219 mm] | 1 in [25.4 mm] | — |

  The general limit is a constant ≈ 2.1 % of span (rise/span); the high-speed limit is ≈ 1.6 % (checked in Python). Because the limit is linear in span, the equivalent FEFCO Warp Factor falls with span: 17.2 at 305 mm and 4.3 at 1219 mm. So the two systems cannot be swapped with one factor. The end user must tell the supplier when the application is high speed [S2].
- Digital presses are reported to tolerate no more than 1/8 in of warp per linear foot [S10]. That is 1.04 %, about 10.4 mm per metre, i.e. half the general FBA limit (WF ≈ 8.5 over 305 mm).
- Worked example: a sheet rising 8 mm over an 800 mm span has 1.0 % warp, WF = 8 × (500/800)² = 3.1.
- Flexo folder-gluer and die-cutter feeders: use the FBA high-speed limit as the default for automatic lines; OEM limits override it.

### Score cracking

- Score cracking is controlled through board moisture and score settings, not through a dimensional tolerance.
- TAPPI guidance reports that total combined-board moisture of about 5.8–7.5 % works well all year [S13].
- See `manufacturing/corrugator-and-converting.md` for causes and remedies.

## Strength test tolerances (ECT, burst)

- ECT (TAPPI T 811 / ISO 3037) and burst results come with a **precision statement** (repeatability and reproducibility) in each method. The current T 811 edition is om-23 [S28]. It gives its figures in Section 9 [S21], but those figures are paywalled and were not retrieved (**VERIFY**).
- As an example of the size of these figures, TAPPI T 809 (flat crush of medium) lists 5 % repeatability and 11 % reproducibility for averages of 10 specimens [S22]. Do not use these numbers for ECT.
- **Practice**: specifications state a **minimum** ECT or burst (lower specification limit), not a ± band. Lot acceptance compares the lot mean and minimum individual values to the spec. Allow for between-lab reproducibility when supplier and customer labs disagree.

## Incoming inspection sampling (ISO 2859-1 / AQL)

- ISO 2859-1 covers sampling by attributes, indexed by acceptance quality limit (AQL), for lot-by-lot inspection. A third edition was published in 2026; many specifications still cite the 1999 edition [S14].
- **Procedure** [S15]:
  1. Choose the inspection level. General Level II is the default; Level III is tighter (for suppliers with recent problems); Level I is looser.
  2. Look up the sample-size **code letter** from lot size and inspection level (Table 1).
  3. Use the code letter and the chosen AQL in the master table (Table 2-A for single sampling, normal inspection) to get the sample size *n*, the acceptance number *Ac* and the rejection number *Re*.
- For corrugated boxes, define defect classes in the house spec (critical, major, minor). For example, major covers dimensions out of tolerance, joint gap or fishtail out of limit, and ECT below minimum; minor covers print and cosmetic defects. Assign an AQL per class.
- AQL values are a contract decision. Store them in `company/`. Do not copy table values from this file: get *n*, *Ac* and *Re* from the standard.

## Worst-case and RSS tolerance stack-up for product fit

### Equation

```
Nominal clearance:       C0  = I_box − P − Σ(insert/wrap thickness)
Worst case:              C_min = C0 − Σ|T_i|      C_max = C0 + Σ|T_i|
RSS (statistical):       T_rss = sqrt( Σ T_i² ) ;  C = C0 ± T_rss
Requirement:             C_min (worst case) ≥ 0 for guaranteed fit; C_max ≤ allowable movement for protection
```

### Variables

- `I_box`: nominal inside dimension of the box, mm [in]
- `P`: nominal product dimension along the same axis, mm [in]
- `T_i`: symmetric ± tolerance of each contributor (box inside, product, inserts), mm [in]
- `C0`, `C_min`, `C_max`: nominal, minimum and maximum clearance, mm [in]

### Validity

- Worst case guarantees fit when every part is at its extreme. It often leads to tight, costly tolerances [S16].
- RSS assumes the contributors are independent, roughly normal and centred. Each T_i is then about 3σ. If those assumptions fail, RSS gives an optimistic answer [S16][S17].
- For fit-critical work (automatic case packing), use worst case. For cost studies, use RSS.

### Worked example (SI)

Box inside L = 400 mm ± 4 mm (FEFCO band 300–700 mm [S1]; the band is defined on crease dimensions, so applying it to the inside size assumes the caliper allowance is constant). Product L = 390 ± 2 mm.

- C0 = 400 − 390 = 10 mm
- Worst case: C_min = 396 − 392 = **4 mm**; C_max = 404 − 388 = **16 mm**
- RSS: T_rss = √(4² + 2²) = 4.47 mm, so C = 10 ± 4.47 mm = **5.5 to 14.5 mm**

### Worked example (US)

Box inside L = 16 in ± 1/8 in. Product L = 15.5 ± 1/16 in.

- Worst case: C_min = 15.875 − 15.5625 = **0.3125 in (5/16 in)** [7.9 mm]; C_max = 16.125 − 15.4375 = **0.6875 in** [17.5 mm]
- RSS: T_rss = √(0.125² + 0.0625²) = 0.140 in [3.55 mm]

### Limitations

- Box dimensions also change with moisture and with bulging under load. Stacking causes panel bulge, which reduces the effective inside size at mid-panel. Static tolerance numbers ignore this.

## Computing worst-case inside-dimension intervals from blank tolerances

```
I = S − k_s · t
I_min = (S − T_S) − k_s · (t + T_t)
I_max = (S + T_S) − k_s · (t − T_t)
```

- `S`: score-to-score panel size, mm [in]
- `T_S`: panel tolerance, mm [in]
- `t`, `T_t`: caliper and its tolerance, mm [in]
- `k_s`: allowance factor (see `dimensional/dimensions-and-allowances.md`)

Worked example (SI). The tolerance inputs are illustrative, not sourced values: S = 404 mm, T_S = ±1.5 mm (the FBA panel value [S2]), t = 4.0 ± 0.3 mm (example input), k_s = 1.

- I_min = 402.5 − 4.3 = **398.2 mm**; I_max = 405.5 − 3.7 = **401.8 mm**

Worked example (US): S = 16 5/32 in, T_S = ±1/16 in, t = 0.156 ± 0.012 in (example input).

- I = **15.926 to 16.075 in** [404.5 to 408.3 mm]

## Product-to-box clearance guidelines

Published guidance varies widely. Treat all of these as starting points and replace them with house data:

| Guidance | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Snug fit, custom boxes | 1/8–1/4 in [3–6 mm] per side | [S18] |
| Rigid retail items | 1/8 in [3 mm] total | [S18] |
| Protective fit (void for cushioning) | 1/2–3/4 in [12.7–19 mm] per side | [S19] |
| Mailer boxes | ~2 mm [0.08 in] each side | [S20] |

**VERIFY**: the brief's "+3–6 mm per dimension" overlaps the snug-fit values in [S18] (1/8–1/4 in per side, i.e. 6–12 mm per dimension) and the mailer value in [S20] (≈4 mm per dimension). There is no single standard. Choose the clearance from the stack-up above (C_min ≥ 0) plus the needs of the packing method (manual or automatic) and the cushion design (see `cushioning/`).
