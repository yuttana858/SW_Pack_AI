# Containerboard paper grades, nomenclature and paper test methods

Scope: the papers that make up corrugated board (liners and fluting/medium), how they are named in Europe and North America, the laboratory tests used to grade them, and published typical values. All numeric property values below are **industry-typical** (taken from individual mill data sheets) — *replace with supplier/house data in `company/`*.

Research note: source documents were consulted through web search extracts; several mill PDFs could not be opened in full. Values whose column mapping was unclear in the extract are marked **VERIFY**.

## Liner and fluting families (overview)

Containerboard splits into **liners** (the flat facings) and **fluting / corrugating medium** (the wavy middle). Fibre origin is the main grade divider [S8][S9]:

| Family | Main fibre | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| Kraftliner (brown, unbleached) | Predominantly virgin (primary) kraft fibre [S9] | Outer/inner liner where strength and wet performance matter |
| White-top kraftliner | Virgin kraft base with bleached top ply [S8] | Printed outer liner |
| Mottled / semi-bleached white top | Virgin or recycled base with partly covered top ply | Lower-cost print liner (**VERIFY**: no source retrieved defining "mottled" grade limits) |
| Testliner (TL1/TL2/TL3), brown | Predominantly recovered fibre [S9] | General-purpose liners |
| White-top testliner | Recovered fibre base with white top ply [S23] | Printed outer liner, recycled |
| Semichemical fluting | Predominantly virgin hardwood (NSSC) fibre [S9][S26] | High-performance fluting |
| Recycled fluting / medium ("Wellenstoff") | Predominantly recovered fibre [S9] | Standard fluting |
| High-performance (HP) recycled fluting | Recovered fibre, strength-enhanced | Lighter-weight fluting at equal CMT/SCT |

The Cepi ContainerBoard "European list of corrugated base papers" is the reference grade classification in Europe; it uses burst index and SCT-CD index as the main strength criteria for liners and SCT-CD / CMT for fluting [S8]. The list is the normative source — consult it for grade boundaries rather than relying on this summary.

## Kraftliner (unbleached brown)

- Made predominantly from primary (virgin) fibre [S9].
- Grammage range offered by one Nordic mill: 100, 115, 125, 135, 170, 186, 200 g/m² [S10].
- Published values at nominal 125 g/m² (industry-typical, two mills):

| Property | SCA Kraftliner [S10] | Saica NK typical [S11] | Saica NK guaranteed [S11] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burst | 560 kPa | 400 kPa | ≥350 kPa |
| SCT-CD | 2.6 kN/m | 2.8 kN/m | ≥2.4 kN/m |
| Cobb 60 | 30 g/m² | 30 g/m² | ≤40 g/m² |
| Moisture basis | not stated | 9.5 % (nominal) | — |

**VERIFY**: burst at 125 g/m² differs by 160 kPa between the two sheets (560 vs 400 kPa). The SCA sheet extracted may be an older edition; burst depends on fibre and test method (ISO 2759 vs ISO 2758 give different results [S1]). Use your supplier's current sheet.

- Burgo BCB Liner 1 (a kraft-type liner, values at 7.5 % moisture basis): 120 g/m² → SCT-CD 2.40 kN/m, burst 346 kPa, Cobb 60 max 30 g/m²; 140 g/m² → SCT-CD 2.80 kN/m, burst 404 kPa [S12].
- Derived SCT-CD index (SCT ÷ grammage): 2.8 kN/m ÷ 125 g/m² ≈ 22.4 kN·m/kg for Saica NK; 2.6 ÷ 125 ≈ 20.8 kN·m/kg for SCA (derived from [S10][S11]).
- A patent describing containerboard states that containerboard materials normally have an MD SCT index above 30 N·m/g [S29] (MD, not CD; 1 N·m/g = 1 kN·m/kg).

## Testliner grades (TL1, TL2, TL3)

Testliners are recycled-fibre liners graded 1–3 by burst and SCT; grade 1 is the strongest [S8][S14]. Palm groups its brown testliners into quality grades 1, 2 and 3 "depending on burst pressure and SCT value" [S14].

Published values at 120 g/m² (Palm specification values; extracted column mapping inferred — **VERIFY** against the PDFs):

| Grade (source) | Burst kPa | Burst index kPa·m²/g | SCT-CD kN/m (avg / min per reel) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Palm TL2, 120 g/m² [S14] | 300 | 2.5 | 2.09 / 1.92 |
| Palm TL3, 120 g/m² [S13] | 240 | 2.0 | 2.00 / 1.90 |
| Burgo BCB Liner 3, 120 g/m², typical (guaranteed) [S16] | 264 (≥238) | — | 1.92 (≥1.73) |
| Palm TL2 at 130 g/m² (product page) [S14] | — | 2.5 | avg 2.3, min 2.1 |

- Palm TL1 120 g/m² values were not readable in the extract (**VERIFY** in [S15]). Palm TL1 specifies moisture 6.5–9 % and Cobb 60 < 35 g/m² on request (issue 2023-06-01) [S15].
- Grammage tolerance on Palm testliners: ±3 % up to 200 g/m², ±4 % above [S13][S14].
- Cepi list extract shows testliner threshold pairs of "burst index ≥ 1.5 or SCT-CD ≥ 12", "≥ 1.3 or ≥ 11", "≥ 1.7 or ≥ 13" but the mapping to TL1/TL2/TL3 and the SCT unit (index, N·m/g) were not legible — **VERIFY** in [S8] before use.
- Derived SCT-CD index: Palm TL3 120 g/m² → 2.00/120 ≈ 16.7 kN·m/kg; Palm TL2 → 2.09/120 ≈ 17.4 kN·m/kg (derived from [S13][S14]).

"Recycled liner" in North America is the analogous recovered-fibre grade, sold by basis weight (see North American nomenclature below) [S25].

## Semichemical fluting

- Made predominantly from primary fibre [S9]; in North America typically NSSC (neutral sulphite semichemical) from hardwoods [S26].
- Billerud Flute S (typical, data sheet rev. 202202 values): 112 g/m² → CMT30 230 N, CCT 1.9 kN/m, SCT-MD 4.7 kN/m, SCT-CD 2.5 kN/m [S17]. Current sheet (rev. 202501) lists 112 / 125 / 140 g/m² with caliper 190 / 215 / 240 µm [S17]. **VERIFY** column alignment (an older sheet listed 127 g/m²).
- Derived SCT-CD index: 2.5 kN/m ÷ 112 g/m² ≈ 22.3 kN·m/kg (derived).
- The Cepi list notes that the correlation between CCT and SCT-CD differs for semichemical fibre versus other fibres [S8], so do not convert CCT↔SCT with recycled-fluting correlations.
- North American NSSC medium example: 23# to 40# basis weight (112–195 g/m²), targets 23, 26, 31, 33, 36, 40 lb/MSF [S26].

## European paper and board naming codes

- Combined board is written outer liner / flute / inner liner, with grammage and a paper-type letter. A published example is **125K/B/125T** = 125 g/m² kraft outer liner, B-flute, 125 g/m² test inner liner [S32][S33].
- Verified letters: **K** = kraftliner (virgin kraft), **T** = testliner (recycled) [S32][S33].
- Other letters seen in converter catalogues — W (white top), S (semichemical fluting), B / M / F used for fluting medium — were **not confirmed** by a retrievable source in this review. **VERIFY** against your converter's grade key; letters are house conventions, not a standard.
- When the fluting grade is written instead of the flute letter (e.g. "125K/112F/125K"), the middle term is the fluting grammage and paper type; the flute profile must then be stated separately. **VERIFY** the meaning of each letter with the supplier — "B" can mean B-flute or a paper code depending on the house.
- Double wall adds a centre liner and second flute: outer / flute 1 / centre liner / flute 2 / inner (format extrapolated from [S32]; **VERIFY** house order).

## Paper test methods and what they predict

Summaries only — consult the standard for procedure. Edition status changes; check the ISO TC 6 catalogue [S7] and TAPPI store before citing an edition.

| Test | ISO | TAPPI | Measures | Predicts / used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SCT (short-span compression) | ISO 9895 | T 826 (om:2021 current per listing) [S4] | In-plane compressive strength of paper over a very short span, MD and CD [S1] | Good estimate of combined-board ECT; main liner/fluting grade criterion in Europe [S1][S8] |
| RCT (ring crush) | ISO 12192 (VERIFY, not retrieved) | T 822 rigid support (0.28–0.61 mm board) [S2]; T 818 flexible beam (0.28–0.51 mm) [S3] | Edgewise compression of a ring-shaped strip | North American liner/medium compression grade; ECT/BCT estimates |
| CMT (Concora medium test) | ISO 7263 | T 809 [S5][S6] | Flat crush of laboratory-fluted medium | Fluting contribution to flat crush and runnability |
| CCT (corrugated crush test) | ISO 16945 [S17] | — | Edgewise compression of laboratory-fluted medium | Alternative to SCT for fluting; correlation differs for semichemical [S8] |
| Burst (paper/liner) | ISO 2759 (ISO 2758 alternative gives lower results) [S1] | T 807 (linerboard) [S1][S5] | Hydraulic bursting pressure | Liner toughness; burst-index grade criterion in Europe [S8] |
| Burst (combined board) | ISO 2759 | T 810 (om-22) [S30] | Bursting pressure of corrugated/solid fibreboard | Mullen-based North American board grades |
| Cobb water absorption | ISO 535 | T 441 [S31] | Water absorbed in a set time (e.g. 60 s), g/m² | Glue-line / printing / humidity behaviour; sizing level |
| Tensile | ISO 1924-2 (constant rate of elongation) | T 494 [S1] | Tensile strength, stretch, TEA | Runnability, tear-off; stiffness modelling |
| Moisture content | ISO 287 (oven drying) | T 412 (identical) [S1] | Mass loss on drying, % | Corrugator warp, conditioning; property values are moisture-dependent |
| Air permeance (porosity) | ISO 5636-5 (Gurley) [S17] | — | Air resistance in seconds | Vacuum-handling, adhesive penetration |

Mill sheets state moisture windows such as 6.5–9 % [S15]; property values are often quoted at a reference moisture basis (e.g. 7.5 % [S12]), so always compare at the same basis.

## How to use these values

1. Treat every number here as a starting estimate, never a specification.
2. Use SCT-CD (Europe) or RCT (North America) of each ply to estimate ECT — see `performance/` files.
3. Record the actual supplier-certified values in `company/materials/` using `materials/material-record-schema.md`; those override this file.
