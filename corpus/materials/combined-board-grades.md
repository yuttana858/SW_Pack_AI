# Combined corrugated board grades

Covers North American carrier-rule grades (UFC Rule 41 for rail, NMFC Item 222 for motor freight) and European grade notation. The official rule texts are paid publications and were **not** retrieved; the tables below come from secondary sources that agree with each other, and must be checked against the current NMFC/UFC before being used for certification or a freight claim. **VERIFY**.

## What Rule 41 and Item 222 are

- NMFC Item 222 (motor carriers, NMFTA) and UFC Rule 41 (rail) set requirements for fibreboard boxes: size and weight limits per board grade, the strength of the fibreboard, and the Box Manufacturer's Certificate (BMC) printed on conforming boxes [S6][S2].
- They are carrier tariff rules, not performance tests; ISTA describes how they differ from ISTA distribution test procedures [S12].
- A box may carry the round BMC only if it meets Item 222; non-compliant boxes may carry a voluntary stamp naming the maker and board test [S6][S7].
- Two alternative qualification routes [S2][S3]:
  - **Table A (burst/Mullen):** minimum bursting test **plus** minimum combined weight of facings (liner basis weights).
  - **Table B (ECT):** minimum edge crush test only; no liner weight requirement.
- The ECT alternative was added after industry proposals around 1990 [S2].
- Shippers are responsible for checking that box contents match the BMC [S7].

## Current status of Item 222 (checked 2026-11-09)

- NMFC Docket 2025-1 moved most classifications to a density basis, effective 19 July 2025 [S10]. No source found states that Item 222 was cancelled.
- NMFTA's Docket 2026-2 analysis still refers to "Boxes, as described in Items 220, 221 and 222" as a packaging category [S8], indicating Item 222 remains in the NMFC.
- Docket 2026-1 (public meeting 10 March 2026) proposed changes to Item 640 (mixed shipments), including a packing requirement for fibreboard boxes on pallets — not to Item 222 itself [S9].
- NMFTA also stresses that packaging choices change density and therefore class [S17].
- **VERIFY**: full texts of Dockets 2025-2, 2026-2 and 2026-3 were not reviewed. Check NMFTA ClassIT+ or the docket PDFs before relying on Item 222 wording. The current status of UFC Rule 41 (rail) was not checked.

## Using carrier-rule grades in design

1. Carrier-rule grades define **minimum material to qualify for carriage**, not stacking performance. Size the board for compression with BCT methods (see `performance/`), then check the resulting grade meets the rule for the box's weight and L+W+D.
2. Specify either the burst route (burst + facings weight) **or** the ECT route; the BMC states which one applies [S2][S6].
3. ECT-certified board may use lighter liners than a burst-certified board of the "equivalent" grade, so puncture/burst performance is not guaranteed [S3].

## European board grades and notation

- Europe does not use carrier-rule grades; board is specified by its paper build-up and measured board properties (ECT, burst, FCT, caliper).
- Notation: outer liner / flute / inner liner with grammage and paper-type letter, e.g. **125K/B/125T** = 125 g/m² kraft outer liner, B-flute, 125 g/m² testliner inner [S16]. See `materials/paper-grades.md` for letter codes.
- Board ECT is measured per ISO 3037 [S15] (edge crush, kN/m). Burst per ISO 2759.
- **Typical ECT (kN/m) for common European combinations was not found** in any retrievable source in this review. Do not use a generic value; obtain the corrugator's certified ECT for each grade or estimate from ply SCT (see `performance/` ECT estimation) and record it in `company/`.
