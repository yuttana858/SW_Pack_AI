# FEFCO / ESBO Code — corrugated and solid board box styles

> **Research note (2026-11-09):** the sources above were consulted through search-engine
> excerpts; the full PDFs (including the 12th-edition FEFCO Code [S4]) could not be opened
> from the research session. Style names and descriptions marked **VERIFY** must be checked
> against the drawing in the official FEFCO Code before being used in a specification or to
> drive CAD. The FEFCO Code is copyrighted: this file summarises it and does not reproduce
> its drawings or text.

## What the FEFCO Code is

The FEFCO Code (formally the FEFCO–ESBO Code, historically titled the "International
Fibreboard Case Code") is a numeric catalogue of box and fitment designs for corrugated
board and solid board [S3][S7][S8]. Each design has a four-digit number, so a buyer and a
converter in different countries can name the same construction without translating style
names [S8]. The numbers identify the **construction** (panels, flaps, joint, closure) only;
they do not specify board grade, flute or size.

- The code is maintained jointly by FEFCO (European Federation of Corrugated Board
  Manufacturers) and ESBO (European Solid Board Organisation); changes can only be made by
  the two bodies together [S8].
- It has been adopted by the ICCA (International Corrugated Case Association), giving it
  worldwide use [S8][S1].
- One source dates the first code to the 1960s, developed by FEFCO and ESBO (then named
  ASSCO) [S8].

## Current edition (12th, 2022)

- The **12th edition** was launched in **2022** [S1][S4]. It was renamed "FEFCO Code — Design
  styles library for corrugated board products" [S1][S4].
- The 12th edition added **more than 100** designs, including a new series for retail and
  e-commerce packaging [S1].
- The **11th edition** was published in **2007** and is the edition most older catalogues and
  supplier PDFs reproduce [S7]. Translations of the 12th edition may lag the English version [S2].
- No 13th edition was found in the search on 2026-11-09; confirm on fefco.org before quoting
  "current edition" in a customer document. **VERIFY**

## Relation between FEFCO and ESBO (solid board)

ESBO is the European Solid Board Organisation. The code covers both corrugated board and
solid (non-fluted) fibreboard, which is why the full name is the FEFCO–ESBO Code [S3][S8].
The same four-digit style number applies whether the case is made from corrugated or solid
board; the material is specified separately. Rigid-type boxes (family 06) and some interior
fittings are more common in solid board, but the code itself does not restrict a style to
one material.

## Structure of the four-digit number

The first two digits are the **family (basic type)**; the last two identify the style within
that family [S8][S12]. Example: 0201 is style 01 in family 02 (slotted-type boxes) [S12].

| Family | Name | What defines it |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Commercial rolls and sheets | Rolls of board (0100) and cut sheets / blanks (0110); not boxes [S12] |
| 02 | Slotted-type boxes | Basically one piece, with a glued, stitched or taped manufacturer's joint and top and bottom flaps; shipped flat, closed with their own flaps [S7] |
| 03 | Telescope-type boxes | More than one piece; a lid and/or bottom telescopes over the body [S7] |
| 04 | Folder-type boxes and trays | Usually one piece; the bottom hinges to form two or all side walls and the cover; locking tabs, handles, display panels possible [S7][S12] |
| 05 | Slide-type boxes | Several liners and sleeves that slide into each other in different directions; also covers outer sleeves [S12] |
| 06 | Rigid-type boxes | Two separate end pieces and a body; need stitching or a similar operation before use [S12] |
| 07 | Ready-glued cases | Basically one piece, shipped flat and ready to use after simple set-up [S12][S13] |
| 08 | (see note) | One source says there is no 08 basic type; another assigns 08 to retail / e-commerce designs. The 12th edition added a retail/e-commerce series [S1] but its family number was not confirmed. **VERIFY** [S12] |
| 09 | Interior fitments | Inserts, liners, pads, partitions, dividers and compartments, either separate or integrated [S12] |

A design can sometimes be classified in more than one family; the code assigns it one number
[S12].

## Erection and closing codes (M, A, M/A)

The code marks each design with how it is erected or closed: **M** = manual and **A** =
automatic; **M/A** means either, and **M+A** a combination [S7][S17]. FEFCO notes that some
manually erected cases can be closed automatically, citing 0216 and 0712 as examples [S7].
Use this flag when checking whether a style will run on a customer's case erector.

## Dimension convention (L × B × H, inside)

- Unless otherwise specified, all dimensions in the FEFCO Code are **internal dimensions in
  millimetres** [S4][S5].
- Dimensions are given in the order **Length (L) × Breadth/Width (B) × Height (H)** [S5].
- **Length** is the longer side of the opening, **Breadth** the shorter side of the opening,
  and **Height** the distance from the top of the opening to the base [S5].
- The order is kept for special formats, so check the drawing rather than assuming the
  largest number is L [S5].
- The difference between inside and outside dimensions depends on board caliper (single,
  double or triple wall); see `dimensional/` for allowances.
- The North American convention (FBA) is also length × width × depth, inside dimensions,
  where L and W are the opening; it is equivalent to FEFCO L × B × H [S6].

## Family 02 — slotted-type boxes

Slotted boxes are one piece, scored and slotted, with a manufacturer's joint that may be
glued, stitched or taped [S7]. They are the workhorse shipping cases. US names follow the
Fibre Box Association (FBA) [S6].

| Code | Name (US / common) | Flap arrangement and closure | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0200 | Half slotted container (HSC) | An 0201 without top flaps; one set of flaps [S6] | Open-top trays, covers, used with a separate lid (see 0320) |
| 0201 | Regular slotted container (RSC) | All flaps the same length; outer flaps meet at the centre, inner flaps normally do not meet [S6][S12] | General shipping case; most common style [S12] |
| 0202 | Overlap slotted container (OSC) | Like 0201 but the outer flaps partially overlap [S6] | Long boxes where the outer flaps alone would not meet the closure tape well |
| 0203 | Full overlap slotted container (FOL) | Both sets of outer flaps overlap to the full width of the box [S6] | Heavy or rough handling; stacking on the flap side; extra flap-area cushioning |
| 0204 | Center special slotted container (CSSC) | Inner and outer flaps cut to different lengths so both pairs meet at the centre [S6] | Full flat closure on both flap pairs |
| 0205 | Center special overlap slotted container | Inner flaps meet; outer flaps partially overlap [S6] | As 0204 with more closure overlap |
| 0206 | Center special full overlap slotted container | Inner flaps meet; outer flaps fully overlap [S6] | As 0204, heavy duty |
| 0209 | — | Slotted type, erection M/A [S17]. Flap geometry not confirmed. **VERIFY** | — |
| 0210 | — | Opposite lid and bottom flaps with insert (tuck) tabs, per one vendor [S18]; erection M [S17]. **VERIFY** | Reclosable shipper |
| 0215 | Slotted box with "Swedish" lock bottom (vendor term) | Four bottom flaps folded in sequence to interlock, no tape [S12] | Fast manual erection without bottom tape |
| 0216 | — | Erection M; FEFCO cites it as manually erected but automatically closable [S7][S17]. Geometry **VERIFY** | — |
| 0225, 0226, 0227, 0229 | — | Slotted-type variants, erection M [S17]. Geometry not confirmed. **VERIFY** | — |
| 0228 | — | Slotted-type variant, erection M/A [S17]. Geometry not confirmed. **VERIFY** | — |

Joint type for all 02 styles is the manufacturer's joint (glued lap, stitched/stapled or
taped), selected in the order, not by the code number [S7].

## Family 03 — telescope-type boxes

Telescope boxes have a body and a separate lid and/or bottom that slides over it [S7].
Covers are usually stitched, glued or locked at the corners.

| Code | Name (US / common) | Construction | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0300 | Telescope / lid box (vendor descriptions disagree) | One vendor calls it a classic lid-type box; another describes two separate pieces that fit together [S16][S17]. **VERIFY** against the code drawing | Gift, retail, shipping |
| 0301 | Full telescope design style box | Two scored and slotted trays; the lid tray is slightly larger and slides over the bottom tray [S9][S6] | Garments, flat goods, variable-height shipper |
| 0306 | Design style container with cover | A slotted tray with a lid; the common "shoe box" design [S9] | Shoes, retail goods |
| 0310 | Double cover box | Body with a separate cover at each end; erection M + A [S7][S9]. Details **VERIFY** | Tall or long products, appliances |
| 0320 | Full telescope half slotted container (FTHSC) | Two half-slotted containers (0200), one telescoping over the other [S9] | Cheap variable-height shipper; lower protection than other designs [S9] |
| 0325 | Interlocking double cover box | Flanges on the body interlock with flanges on the covers and are held by strapping; erection A [S9][S7] | Heavy goods on pallets, appliances |

## Family 05 — slide-type boxes

Slide-type boxes are several pieces (trays, liners and sleeves) that slide into each other
[S12].

| Code | Name | Construction | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0501 | Slide box (sleeve + tray) | Geometry of 0501 vs 0502 not confirmed. **VERIFY** | Drawer-style retail, protective sleeving |
| 0502 | Slide box variant | Second style in the slide family [S12]; geometry **VERIFY** | — |
| 0503 | Outer sleeve / slipcase | Open-ended sleeve used as an outer cover or band; used with a 0907 inner part in one supplier's product [S12] | Banding, slipcases, promotional covers |

## Family 06 — rigid-type boxes

Rigid-type boxes (in the FEFCO sense, not the paper-wrapped "set-up" boxes of the luxury
trade) are a body plus two separate end pieces, stitched or otherwise joined before use
[S12]. Codes 0601 and 0605 are in this family; their individual geometry was not confirmed
from the sources consulted. **VERIFY** These styles are common in solid board (ESBO) for
heavy, returnable or wet-strength packs.

## Family 07 — ready-glued cases

Ready-glued cases are one piece, glued by the converter, shipped flat and erected by a simple
movement, often with an automatic ("crash-lock") bottom [S12][S13].

Interior fitments are liners, pads, partitions and dividers, either separate or integrated
into the box [S12]. They are specified with the outer case (for example "0201 + 0933").

## How to use FEFCO codes in a specification

1. Give the code, the inside dimensions in L × B × H order in mm, the board grade, the flute
   and the manufacturer's-joint type (glued, stitched or taped).
2. Say whether dimensions are inside (FEFCO default) or outside; never assume [S4][S5].
3. Name any fitment by its 09 code, together with the outer case code.
4. Check the M / A erection flag against the customer's line [S7].
5. For any style marked **VERIFY** above, attach the converter's drawing; the code number
   alone is not enough until the geometry is confirmed.
