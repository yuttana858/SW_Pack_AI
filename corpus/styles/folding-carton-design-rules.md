# Folding carton structural design rules

Design rules for printed, die-cut, folder-glued paperboard cartons. This file does **not**
repeat material already in the corpus; it cross-references it:

- Style codes (ECMA A/B groups) and the ECMA **A × B × H crease-centre convention**: `styles/ecma-folding-carton.md`.
- Board grades, caliper units, stiffness tests, and the **grain-direction convention**: `materials/paperboard-carton.md`.
- CITO creasing-rule height and channel-width equations, nicks, ejection rubber, CAD line types: `manufacturing/die-cutting-and-tooling.md`.
- Corrugated allowances, glue-lap minimums and RSC tolerances: `dimensional/dimensions-and-allowances.md`, `dimensional/blank-size-formulas.md`, `dimensional/tolerances.md`.
- Test-method editions (ISO 187, TAPPI T 402, ASTM D642, ISO 12048, TAPPI T 804): `testing/standards-index.md`.

**Confidence.** Machine limits (BOBST) and tooling charts (CITO) are OEM data taken from the
primary pages. Most flap-size and margin numbers come from converter and supplier pages, read
only through search extracts because those hosts are blocked (see Research log); they are
**industry-typical** — *replace with supplier/house data in `company/`*. Geometry stated as
"derived" follows from caliper arithmetic, not from a published standard.

## Dimension bases and caliper allowance for cartons

Three bases are in use; every carton drawing must say which one it uses.

| Base | Definition | Who uses it |
|---|---|---|
| Inside (ID) | Clear space between inner board surfaces | North American L × W × D quotes; product-fit checks |
| Crease-centre (CC) | Distance between crease centre lines on the flat blank | ECMA A × B × H [S1]; most CAD panel dimensions |
| Outside (OD) | Over the outer board surfaces | Shipper-case fit, shelf and cartoner magazine checks; ECMA notes outside sizes matter for transit-case packing [S1] |

### Equation (body panels)

```
CC_i = ID_i + k_i · t        OD_i = ID_i + 2 · t       (i = L or A, W or B)
CC_H = ID_H + k_H · t
```

- `t`: board caliper, mm [in] (1 pt = 0.001 in = 25.4 µm; see `materials/paperboard-carton.md`)
- `k_i`: body-panel allowance factor, **1** (half a caliper at each crease, because the crease centre sits at mid-thickness). One dieline guide states the same rule: crease-to-crease = inside + one board thickness = outside − one board thickness [S24].
- `k_H`: height (closure-to-closure) factor. **1** gives clear height at the carton centre; **2** is the convention of at least one carton CAD tool (Packmage adds one caliper to length and width and two to depth) [S24], which leaves room for the dust-flap and tuck layers that lie inside each end closure (derived). **VERIFY** against the converter's house standard; conventions differ [S24].

Validity: solid board 0.25–1.25 mm; for micro-flute use the corrugated allowance model in
`dimensional/dimensions-and-allowances.md`. Product clearance is a separate, added term
(house data).

### Worked example (SI)

Product 60 × 30 × 120 mm, clearance 1.0 mm per dimension (example input), t = 0.45 mm (≈ 17.7 pt).

- ID = 61.0 × 31.0 × 121.0 mm
- CC (k = 1, k_H = 1) = 61.45 × 31.45 × 121.45 mm; with k_H = 2, CC_H = 121.90 mm
- OD of the body = 61.90 × 31.90 mm

### Worked example (US)

Product 2.375 × 1.250 × 4.750 in, clearance 0.040 in (example input), t = 0.018 in (18 pt).

- ID = 2.415 × 1.290 × 4.790 in
- CC (k = 1) = 2.433 × 1.308 × 4.808 in; with k_H = 2, CC_H = 4.826 in
- OD of the body = 2.451 × 1.326 in

(All values computed with Python; 2.375 in = 60.33 mm, 0.018 in = 0.457 mm.)

Limitations: the 1 t rule assumes a well-formed crease; a shallow crease or a rolled fold moves
the effective hinge outward. Confirm with a sample carton.

## Manufacturer's joint and glue flap

**Placement.** On a long-seam-glued (ECMA group A) tube the glue flap is attached to one end
panel of the panel chain (glue flap – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4); ECMA numbers panels 1–4 starting from the
glue seam [S1]. The joint normally lands on a corner on a narrow panel or on the back, out of the
main display face (practice; see `styles/ecma-folding-carton.md` for the panel numbering).

**Caliper compensation.** The panel at the free end of the chain (opposite the glue flap) is drawn
slightly shorter than its twin, because the glue flap sits inside it and the overlapping layers
add thickness; one dieline guide calls this the thickness offset (B2 slightly shorter than B1)
[S23]. A compensation of about **1 t** follows from the layer count (derived). **VERIFY** the
exact value with the CAD standard in use.

**Glue-flap width (industry-typical).**

| Value | Context | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 8–25 mm glued edge (one supplier's offered range; another quotes 7–25 mm glued edge, 6–25 mm closing flap) | Tuck-end folding boxes | [S22] |
| 20–50 mm adhesive tabs | Crash-lock base boxes | [S21] |
| ≥ 32 mm [1 1/4 in] | **Corrugated** glued joints, not cartons | see `dimensional/blank-size-formulas.md` |

- The glue flap is commonly tapered at its free corners; a CAD style-making tutorial draws it at 15° with an adjustable offset (example, not a rule) (practice).
- Keep varnish, UV coating and ink out of the glue flap and the mating strip (knock-out): adhesive does not bond reliably to varnished or metallised surfaces, and a joint that fails in the coating is unacceptable even if adhesion to the coating is high [S30][S31].
- Glue-line position relative to the flap edges is set on the folder-gluer (for example, the distance to the leading and trailing edges is a controller setting on one bottom-applied side-seam system) — no published mm value was found; use house data.

### Worked example: blank width of a tuck-end tube

Using the SI example above (CC 61.45 × 31.45 mm) and a 15 mm glue flap (example input):
blank width across the panel chain ≈ 15 + 2 × 61.45 + 2 × 31.45 = **200.8 mm** before the
free-end panel compensation (derived). US: 0.590 + 2 × 2.433 + 2 × 1.308 = **8.072 in**.

## Tuck flaps, dust flaps and tuck locks

**Geometry (ECMA terms).** For a tuck end, ECMA's A is the base dimension parallel to the hinge
of the flap that closes last (the tuck flap); B is the other base dimension [S1]. So:

- The **closing panel** (lid) spans B and is hinged on the A-wide panel (derived).
- The **tuck-in flap** is hinged on the free edge of the closing panel and runs A wide (derived).
- The **dust flaps** are hinged on the two B-wide side panels and fold in first, under the closing panel (derived).

**Rules of thumb (low authority — VERIFY with house standard).**

| Rule | Value | Source / basis |
|---|---|---|
| Tuck-in flap length | "at least 75 % of the box depth" (single blog source; "depth" here is the closing-panel span B) | [S45] |
| Tuck-in flap width | A minus a relief at each end (about 1 t per side), so the tuck enters between the side panels | derived |
| Tuck flap corners | Radiused or angled so the tuck guides into the opening; radius is a CAD parameter, no published value found | practice |
| Dust flap length | Each ≤ A/2 so opposing dust flaps cannot clash (derived); usually shorter, and clear of the tuck-in flap | derived |
| Dust flap shape | Leading edge angled or radiused so it does not snag the product or the tuck | practice |

**Friction fit vs slit lock.** A friction-fit tuck relies on a snug fit with no locking feature;
a slit lock adds a mechanical catch where short slits at the ends of the tuck hinge engage the
shoulders of the dust flaps. Suppliers describe the slit lock as more secure and the friction
fit as easier to open and reclose; a common combination is friction lock on top and slit lock on
the bottom [S43]. No published slit length or shoulder geometry was found (**VERIFY**, house CAD
standard). Tab length and angle should be confirmed on a prototype [S45].

**Straight vs reverse tuck** is a style choice (ECMA codes A20.20.01.01 vs A20.20.03.01); see
`styles/ecma-folding-carton.md`.

## Crease design: rule, channel, depth and reverse creases

Primary equations (CITO Abacus, cardboard): creasing-rule height **h = 23.80 − c**, channel width
**B = 1.5·c + d**, channel depth **A ≈ c**, where c = board thickness and d = rule width, all in mm
[S3]. These are already documented with variables and US examples in
`manufacturing/die-cutting-and-tooling.md`; the CITO page states the Abacus is designed for
material thickness 0.2–1.65 mm (its selection list starts at 0.25 mm) and that the parameters
depend on the material specification [S3].

**Rule selection by caliper.**

- Never crease a board with a rule thinner than the board; change from a 2-pt (0.7 mm) to a 3-pt (1.05 mm) rule at about 0.6 mm board [S15].
- HS CITO creasing rules come as 2, 3 and 4 pt, single- or double-round profile, heights 22.40–23.90 mm (2 pt), 21.00–23.90 mm (3 pt), 22.00–23.90 mm (4 pt) [S9]; BOBST lists a 23.8 mm cutting-rule height on its 106 die cutter [S14].
- One board maker's brochure gives the groove depth as matrix thickness − 0.1 mm with a 23.8 mm cutting rule, and a groove-width table by caliper (table values not retrieved) [S40].

**Crease quality targets.**

- A crease should be "as deep and narrow as possible" for accurate folding [S41].
- When the crease runs **parallel to the fibres** (grain), the channel should be narrower than for a crease across the grain [S35].
- Reverse-side (bead-side) cracking stays low (< 5 % of crease length) up to a critical penetration and then rises sharply; a wider rule–channel clearance moves the critical depth deeper. The authors collapse the data with a "creasing draw" parameter and define the critical draw at 5 % cracking [S16].
- The bead should be symmetric; crease-profile instruments measure width, height and symmetry of the crease and both folding edges (one device quotes 0.012 mm width-measurement tolerance) [S10].
- In tray press forming, groove-wall angle and groove width relative to rule width had only a minor effect on corner folding [S29].

### Reverse creases

Used for 180° folds when die cutting from the unprinted side (in-line die cutting), and for the
bottom fold of 4- and 6-point (corner) boxes [S7]. CITO recommends mounting the creasing rules
reversed in the cutting die [S4]. Rule height: **h = 23.8 − c − base height**, with base height
0.7 mm or 0.3 mm depending on the matrix series [S4].

| Board c (mm) | Base 0.7 mm series: size, channel width (mm) | Base 0.3 mm series: size, channel width (mm) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25–0.35 | 2P/12, 1.2–1.4 | 2P/12, 1.2–1.4 |
| 0.40–0.65 | 2P/15, 1.5–1.9 | 2P/15, 1.5–1.9 |
| 0.60–0.85 / 0.65–0.85 | 3P/19, 1.9–2.3 | 3P/23, 2.3–2.7 |
| 0.85–1.10 / 0.80–1.00 | 3P/23, 2.3–2.7 | 2P/23, 2.3–2.7 |
| 1.25–1.45 / 1.05–1.30 | 4P/35, 3.5–4.0 | 4P/35, 3.5–4.0 |

Extract of the two CITO PLAST Reverse Crease charts [S4]; consult the page for the full chart
(the series overlap and include more sizes).

### Worked example (SI and US)

Board c = 0.45 mm [0.0177 in], 2-pt rule d = 0.71 mm [0.028 in]:

- Normal crease: B = 1.5 × 0.45 + 0.71 = **1.385 mm** [0.0546 in]; h = 23.80 − 0.45 = **23.35 mm** [0.919 in]; channel depth ≈ 0.45 mm [S3].
- Reverse crease (2P/15 matrix, 0.40–0.65 mm range): h = 23.8 − 0.45 − 0.7 = **22.65 mm** [0.892 in] on the 0.7 mm-base series, or 23.8 − 0.45 − 0.3 = **23.05 mm** [0.907 in] on the 0.3 mm-base series [S4].

Limitations: starting values only; trial creases and fold tests decide the setting [S3].

## Minimum distances between creases, cuts and features

| Feature | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Two parallel creases (double crease) | Matrix channel centre distances 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 5.0 mm, for 200–300 g/m² board (0.25–0.30 mm) | [S6] |
| Crease close to another crease or to a cut | Use an off-centre matrix: OFF 40/50/60 for 0.15–0.20 / 0.25–0.30 / 0.40–0.55 mm board, matrix widths 1.00 / 1.30 / 1.60 mm; rule height = 23.8 − c − 0.1 mm | [S5] |
| Cut close to crease (glue flaps, window cuttings, small cartons) | Use a narrow ("MINI") matrix | [S8] |
| Braille dots to centre of any cut or crease line | 8 mm, measured from the dot edge | [S2] |
| Window cut path to fold and cut lines | ≥ 15 mm (single supplier guide) | [S28] |
| Cut-out (holder) to crease | ≥ 10 mm (same guide) | [S28] |
| Adjacent steel rules in the die | ≥ 1.5 mm (single vendor blog; **VERIFY** with die maker) | [S46] |

Derived consequence: with a standard (centred) matrix, two creases or a crease and a cut cannot
be closer than about half the matrix width plus the rule half-width; below that, specify the
off-centre or MINI matrix rather than shrinking the panel (derived from [S5][S8]).

ECMA Braille also sets the minimum carton size for a Braille panel: 22.6 / 32.6 / 42.6 / 52.6 mm
for 1–4 lines (A or B dimension) and 50.1 mm for 6 characters up to 134.1 mm for 20 characters
per line (H dimension), at 6.0 mm character pitch and 10.0 mm (+0/−0.1 mm) line spacing [S2].

## Grain direction by carton type

The corpus convention is in `materials/paperboard-carton.md`: **default horizontal grain**
(around the tube, perpendicular to the body creases) for side-gripped, end-loading tuck and
sleeve cartons; **vertical grain** where top load on the upright carton governs. Additional
creasing facts that bear on the choice:

- Board folds more easily and cracks less when the fold line runs with the grain; folding across the grain is the higher-risk orientation for cracking [S37].
- A crease parallel to the grain needs a narrower channel than one across the grain [S35], so on one die the two crease directions may need different matrices.
- For trays, crash-lock bottoms and 1-2-3 bottoms no published grain rule was found. Apply the load-case logic of the convention: the panel that must resist bulging or top load should have its stiffer (MD) direction spanning the critical bending direction (derived). **VERIFY** with the board supplier.
- Mark grain on every dieline; if sheet nesting forces the other direction, the converter must flag it (see `materials/paperboard-carton.md`).

## Crash-lock (auto) bottom and 1-2-3 (snap-lock) bottom

**Codes.** Crash-lock = ECMA closure value 60 (automatic, glued at two points), tuck top crash-lock
A60.20.00.03; 1-2-3 / snap-lock = value 55 (self-locking, unglued), A55.20.01.03 [S1]. Details in
`styles/ecma-folding-carton.md`.

**Crash-lock geometry (derived).** Each of the two glued bottom-flap pairs folds on a diagonal
crease from the body corner (45° for a rectangular base when the two flaps of a pair are equal),
so that squaring the flat tube swings the pre-glued flaps into place. Each hooked major flap must
reach past the base centre line, so its depth ≥ B/2 + engagement overlap. Example: CC B = 31.45 mm,
5 mm overlap (example input) → flap depth ≥ 31.45/2 + 5 = **20.7 mm** [0.82 in]. The glue patches
sit on the triangular webs and are applied by the crash-lock module of the folder-gluer [S11][S13].

**Size limits.**

| Limit | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Crash-lock base, minimum carton length and width | 25 mm [0.98 in]: below that the bottom flaps are too small for a secure glue area (one supplier) | [S21] |
| Crash-lock on a BOBST EXPERTFOLD/NOVAFOLD | blank C min 146 mm [5.74 in]; folded width L min 70 mm [2.75 in]; max C 500/800/1100 mm by machine width | [S11][S12] |
| 1-2-3 bottom | unglued, folded by hand; slower to erect than a crash-lock (see ECMA file) | [S1] |

The two minimums measure different things (finished carton size vs folded-flat width on the
machine) and are not in conflict; check both.

## Windows and window patching

- Keep the window cut at least 15 mm from fold and cut lines, mainly so the film has a glue margin (single supplier guide) [S28].
- Narrow features between a window cut and a crease need a narrow matrix [S8].
- Keep ink and varnish out of the film glue margin, as for any glued area [S30][S31].
- Film overlap width and patcher glue margin were **not** found in a primary source — use the patcher OEM and adhesive supplier data (house data).
- ECMA code group X covers auxiliary features such as windows; add the fifth pair to the style code [S1].

## Folder-gluer constraints

Straight-line boxes on BOBST EXPERTFOLD / NOVAFOLD 50 | 80 | 110 [S11][S12]:

| Dimension | Min | Max (50 / 80 / 110) |
|---|---|---|
| C (blank, across the machine) | 126 mm [4.96 in]; 76 mm [3.00 in] with minimum folding device | 500 / 800 / 1100 mm |
| E (blank, in the running direction) | 60 mm [2.36 in] | 800 mm |
| L (folded flat width) | 60 mm [2.36 in]; 35 mm [1.37 in] with minimum folding device | 235 / 385 / 535 mm |

- Letter meanings (C across, E along, L folded width) are our reading of the BOBST drawings; the brochure gives letters on a sketch only (**VERIFY**).
- Board: carton or solid board up to 800 g/m² [0.049 in, 1.24 mm]; corrugated N, F, E flute; folded box thickness max 12 mm [0.47 in] [S11][S12].
- Speeds: NOVAFOLD 20–350 m/min, up to 18 000 boxes/h; EXPERTFOLD 20–450 m/min, up to 28 000 boxes/h; MASTERFOLD 75/110 20–700 m/min, up to 50 000 boxes/h [S11][S12][S13].
- **Prebreak:** the folder-gluers prebreak (fold to 180° and reopen) the long creases before gluing, so the creases fold accurately and the flat carton opens easily on the cartoner [S12][S13]. Design creases that will be prebroken; avoid perforations or cut-crease lines on prebroken creases unless the prebreak is set for them (practice).
- **Squareness:** a manufacturer's joint that is not square (fishtail) is often traced to a worn folder-gluer, not to the die [S36].
- Max board thickness conflict: Wikipedia states folding-carton board is limited to 0.81 mm (0.032 in) by cutting machinery [S34]; OEM data allow up to 0.049 in (1.24 mm) on the folder-gluer [S11] and CITO crease tables run to 1.65 mm [S3]. **VERIFY**: treat 0.81 mm as an old rule of thumb, not a machine limit.

### Crease-to-board stiffness ratio

```
R_crease = M_crease / M_board
```

- `M_crease`: bending moment (or force) to fold the creased specimen to the test angle, mNm [gf·cm]
- `M_board`: same test on an uncreased specimen of the same direction, mNm [gf·cm]
- Lower R = easier, more predictable fold; DIN 55437-3's folding factor expresses this weakening [S17] (the exact DIN formula was not retrieved).

Example (illustrative): M_crease = 4.0 mNm, M_board = 10.0 mNm → R = 0.40. In gf·cm (1 mNm =
10.197 gf·cm): 40.8 / 102.0 = 0.40 — the ratio is unit-free.

No published acceptance threshold was found. One patent targets a **transverse-to-longitudinal
crease stiffness ratio** of 1.00–1.25 (most preferred 1.00–1.10), adjusted by crease depth or
groove width, so that MD and CD creases fold alike [S42] (**VERIFY**: patent, not a standard).

## Common failure modes and fixes

| Failure | Likely causes | Fixes | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crease cracking (outer liner or coating) | Crease too deep for the clearance; folding across grain; dry board (winter); channel too wide or worn matrix | Reduce penetration or widen clearance below the critical draw; fold with the grain where possible; condition board; replace matrix; check rule-to-channel centring | [S16][S37][S38] |
| Asymmetric bead / fold off the crease line | Rule not centred in the channel; tool tolerance mismatch | Re-centre matrix; measure crease profile | [S10] |
| Gaping or skewed tube (fishtail) | Joint not square; worn folder-gluer; uneven belt speeds | Rebuild/adjust folder-gluer; check die squareness | [S36] |
| Pop-open of glued seam (hot melt) | Cold failure (fracture lines in the glue) or heat failure (bubbles, stringing); poor compression | Correct glue temperature, open time and compression | [S32] |
| Glue joint fails in the coating | Varnish or coating on the glue area; coating bond weaker than adhesive bond | Knock out varnish/UV on the glue strip; choose adhesive for the topcoat; require fibre tear | [S30][S31] |
| Tuck pops out / lid will not stay closed | Tuck too short; friction fit too loose; caliper lower than design | Lengthen tuck; add slit lock; re-check caliper allowance | [S43][S45] |
| Warp / curl of blanks or cartons | Moisture gradient through the thickness; coating asymmetry; temperature/RH swings in transit and storage | Acclimatise board and blanks before converting; control RH; balance coatings | [S33] |
| Carton will not open on the cartoner | High opening force; creases not prebroken | Prebreak 180° on the folder-gluer; test opening force | [S12][S19][S20] |
